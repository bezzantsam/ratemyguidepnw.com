# Arkansas Lake Ouachita sources

Checked 2026-11-25. Phase `arkansas-second-region`. This note extends the Cotter inventory to Lake Ouachita. It records incremental license context, the reuse terms opened for this water, and a four-row sample. People, telephone numbers, email addresses, street addresses, and postal boxes stay outside this repository.

The statewide credential, Guide ARK's unfiltered count, and the Cotter sample stay in `inventory.md`. Those rows are not counted again here.

No one was contacted. This file is not a listing and not a publishing decision.

## License check

The fishing-license fee table was opened again on 2026-11-25. It still lists the resident fishing guide license (GLF) at $250, the resident combination guide license (GLC) at $350, and the nonresident fishing guide license (GNF) at $500. GLF and GNF do not include fishing privileges. The table still has no holder list, bulk file, or field dictionary. The number of fishing-guide licenses on Lake Ouachita is unknown, and the statewide count remains unknown.

A trout permit is required to fish the named tailwaters below Beaver Lake, Bull Shoals Lake, Norfork Lake, Greers Ferry Lake, and Lake Greeson, or east of Highway 59 on Spavinaw Creek, or to keep trout from any Arkansas water. Lake Ouachita is absent from that named-tailwater list. Keeping trout taken from the lake would still require the permit. The resident fisheries conservation license remains $10.50 and the nonresident annual fishing license remains $60. Resident and nonresident trout permits remain $10 and $20.

The guide service is held because a single publisher page names a boat class and no second publisher confirms the operation.

The state-parks host redirects to the Arkansas.com page for Lake Ouachita State Park. That page calls the lake Arkansas' largest and gives its area as 40,000 acres. Species named there are bream, crappie, catfish, striped bass, and largemouth bass. The marina rents boats and sells bait and supplies. Rental classes, with tax and fuel extra where the page says so, are a 22-foot barge with a 90 hp motor at $350 for eight hours, $315 for six hours, and $275 for a half day, deposit $50; a 26-foot tri-toon party barge with an upper deck and a 150 hp motor at $500, $465, and $440, deposit $50; a flat-bottom boat at $85, $72.50, and $60; a canoe at $40, $25, and $12.50 an hour; and a transient slip at $20 a day. Solo and tandem kayaks are offered through a marina kiosk, and that block states no rate. Park boat tours, eagle cruises, and kayak programs are interpretive programs. They are a separate product from a sport-fishing guide roster. The page publishes no guide list. Other publishers' acreage and shoreline figures were left unused because those pages were not opened.

Mountain Harbor Resort and Spa is supported as a marina on its own marina page. The page calls the marina the largest in Arkansas. That sentence is the operator's claim. The 2026 rental block, with fuel extra and tax extra, lists an 18-foot fishing boat that seats 3 at $499 full day and $459 half day; a 26-foot tritoon with a slide and a 200 hp motor that seats 8 at $795 and $745; a 30-foot tritoon with a slide and a 200 hp motor that seats 12 at $845 and $795; and a 26-foot ski barge that seats 8 at $895 and $840. The introductory sentence also names 26-foot and 30-foot Aloha party barges, a 26-foot Aloha ski barge, and an 18-foot Bass Cat. Those brand sentences were not joined to the rate cards, and the repeated cards were not counted as extra hulls. Double kayaks are $70 and $60, and single kayaks and paddle boards are $60 and $50. An on-site credit-card charge is 3 percent. An online reservation charge is 2.6 percent. Later cards repeat the 3 percent line. Life jackets are included. Extra jackets are $5 and ski vests are $10. A driver's license and a credit card are required. The renter is responsible for damage, including props and lower units. Half-day rentals are unavailable on summer weekends and holidays. A deposit is refunded, less a $100 service charge, when the cancellation reaches the office 14 days before arrival. Inside 14 days the deposit is non-refundable. The reservation-policy link did not resolve to its own URL, and the third-party booking catalog was not opened.

The same resort's fishing page says the marina sells fishing licenses. It then names two recommended guides and also calls them the resort's professional fishing guides. The page states no trip price and no hull for those guides. Their names and telephone numbers were not copied. They were not sampled, and they were not matched to Hot Springs Fishing Guide Service. Cooking a guest's catch in the restaurant is hospitality, not a guide product.

Safe Harbor Brady Mountain is supported as a marina on its own location page, at Royal on Lake Ouachita. Wet-slip limits on that page are length overall 30 to 100 feet, maximum height 25 feet, beam 20 feet, and draft 6.5 feet. Transient slips are up to 30 feet, with unrestricted height, beam 11 feet, and draft 6.5 feet. Dry storage is listed at 40 feet length overall. Those figures are facility limits. The page states no slip count and no hull identifier. Rental boats are offered March through October through local rental partners. That partner catalog was not opened, so the rental classes and any guide product inside it are unknown. The page lists vessel service, fuel, a ship's store, and a waterside restaurant. It publishes no fishing-guide roster. A distance to Lake Ouachita State Park on the same page was not used as a cross-check of either rental fleet.

Hot Springs Fishing Guide Service is held. Its own page sells half-day trips of five hours at $700 for one or two people, $800 for three, $900 for four, and $1,050 for five. It says a party of six or more can be arranged with other guides. Those other guides are an unknown count and were not sampled. The fishing license is excluded and the reader is sent to AGFC. Rods, reels, tackle, and bait are included. The about text says striper guiding began in 2004 and describes more than 20 years of the service. The boat block describes a 24-foot Blazer Bay center console with a Minn Kota trolling motor and two graphs. That is a class. The page gives no hull name, HIN, or official number. The page links Mountain Harbor and a second resort as lodging. The second resort was not opened. No second publisher was opened.

No unique hull was identified. No Cotter observation was found on these pages. `arkansas-inventory-cotter-trout-dock`, `arkansas-inventory-ember-shoals`, `arkansas-inventory-rising-river`, and `arkansas-inventory-cotter-elite` stay on the White River sample.

Add a hull only when a public record names that hull with dated physical evidence.
