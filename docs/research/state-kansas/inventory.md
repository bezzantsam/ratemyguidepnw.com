## What is licensed

The assigned start page, `https://www.ksoutdoors.gov/outdoor-activities/fishing-in-kansas`, returned HTTP 403. The same denial came back for `https://ksoutdoors.gov/Fishing/Fishing-Application-and-Fees` and for the Milford State Park page at `https://www.ksoutdoors.gov/State-Parks/Locations/Milford`. The department's own fee page, roster, and park terms were not read. The Secretary of State regulation viewer at `pubs_kar_Regs.aspx` returned HTTP 404. `https://rules.ks.gov/` returned an application shell with no regulation text.

K.S.A. 32-964, on the Revisor of Statutes site, is repealed. The history line is L. 2005, ch. 124, § 4, January 1, 2006. The 2005 session law, Chapter 124, House Bill 2466, approved April 12, 2005, is titled as an act repealing the state's authority to issue commercial guide service permits. Section 4 repeals K.S.A. 2004 Supp. 32-964 on January 1, 2006.

Before that repeal, the 2003 session law, Chapter 108, defined commercial guide services as providing, offering, arranging, or assisting with big game or migratory waterfowl hunting or fishing activities for others on a commercial basis. The permit was issued to a person. It expired on December 31. The secretary could publish a list of permittees only with each permittee's written consent. That section is the repealed text. It is not a current roster.

K.A.R. 115-21-1, 115-21-2, and 115-21-4, as displayed by the Cornell Legal Information Institute, carry notes that each was revoked on January 1, 2006. K.A.R. 115-21-3 carries a note that it was revoked on December 7, 2001. Article 21 is still titled Commercial guides. K.A.R. 115-8-24, commercial guiding on department lands and waters, carries a note that it was revoked effective August 1, 2016. The old text of that section was not on the page.

K.S.A. 32-988, history through L. 2026, ch. 100, § 3, July 1, still lists a commercial guide permit or associate guide permit among the fees the secretary may set. The resident maximum is $250. The nonresident maximum is $1,000. Those figures are statutory caps. The Cornell copy of K.A.R. 115-2-1, whose notes say the last amendment was effective December 29, 2023, prints angler and commercial prices and does not print a commercial guide permit price. A later department HTML fee page was not readable.

K.S.A. 32-906 requires a valid Kansas fishing license to fish or to take any bullfrog, with stated exceptions. The exceptions on the opened section include a person, and an immediate family member domiciled with that person, on land that person owns or leases or rents for agriculture; a person under 16; a resident who is 75 or older; fishing in a private water fishing impoundment unless waived; a resident of a licensed adult care home; two free-fishing days the secretary may designate; an institutional group fishing license; and a department-sponsored fishing clinic. The fee is the amount set under K.S.A. 32-988. Unless a statute or rule says otherwise, a fishing license is valid from issuance and expires on December 31. The secretary may issue a permanent license, a lifetime license, a five-day nonresident license, and a 24-hour license.

The Cornell copy of K.A.R. 115-2-1 prices the annual resident fishing license, valid for one year from purchase, at $25, and the annual nonresident fishing license at $75. A resident calendar-day license is $3.50. A nonresident calendar-day license is $10. A five-day nonresident license is $25. A resident senior fishing license, ages 65 through 74, is $12.50. A five-year resident fishing license is $100. A lifetime fishing license is $500, or eight quarterly payments of $67.50. A trout permit is $12, and a youth trout permit is $4.50. Those amounts sit within the caps read on the current 32-988 page. K.S.A. 32-989 allows a service charge of up to $1 on a license sold by the secretary, a county clerk, or an appointed agent, and a negotiated service charge for a telephone or internet agent. The commercial block of the same fee regulation lists mussel, Missouri River fishing, fish-bait, and prairie-rattlesnake permits. Those are separate from a sport-fishing guide permit.

No public guide roster, download, field dictionary, or issued-permit count was opened. The statewide count of people guiding sport fishing is unknown. The repealed publication rule required written consent and is not a current directory grant.

Thunderbird Marina and RV Resort is supported as a landing. Its own site says it is the largest marina on Milford Lake, on the south end near the dam, and that Milford Lake is about 15,700 acres with 163 miles of shoreline, built by the Army Corps of Engineers on the Republican River. The same pages say the resort season for RV sites and boat slips is April 1 through October 31, that there are 87 slips on A and B docks, and that covered slips run 20, 24, 26, and 30 feet. The Corps page that could have checked the 15,700-acre sentence returned HTTP 403. Deposit amounts on the marina pages are $300 for a site or a slip rented alone, and $150 more for the slip when a site and a slip are rented together.

Scattered Acres Outfitting LLC is held. That email sentence is about visitors. The terms page has no revision date. The footer is 2026 Photos & Text Copyright, Scattered Acres Outfitting LLC. The terms do not grant a directory.

## Where to start

The current fee regulation that was readable prints angler prices and does not print a guide price. For Milford Lake, the two opened landings are Milford State Park Marina and Thunderbird Marina and RV Resort. Keep each landing, each rental class, and any later guide offer separate.
