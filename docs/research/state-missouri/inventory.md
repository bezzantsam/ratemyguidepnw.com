## What is licensed

The starting page is the Missouri Department of Conservation fishing-permit catalog at `https://mdc.mo.gov/permits/fishing-permits`. It is an angler permit list. It does not list guides, boats, or a downloadable roster.

Accepting a permit on that page means the holder agrees to the Wildlife Code of Missouri, will not lend the permit, will allow inspection of the permit, picture identification, catch, and equipment, and will carry the permit while fishing. A buyer who has ordered by telephone or online and has not received the card carries the authorization number and picture identification until the permit arrives.

Fees read on that catalog: Daily Fishing Permit $9 for residents and nonresidents; Fishing Permit $14 resident and $57 nonresident; Trout Permit $12 resident, $24 nonresident, and $6 for youth ages 0–15. The trout permit is required to possess trout, except in trout parks where a daily trout fishing tag is required. It is also required for winter fishing in trout parks and for fishing year-round in Lake Taneycomo upstream from the U.S. Highway 65 bridge. A fishing permit, or an exemption, is still required. The lifetime-permit notes say Missouri residents 65 and older are exempt from a fishing permit and may buy a lifetime trout permit. Those lifetime products are sold to residents and are not sold online.

The same catalog lists the White River Border Lakes Permit at $10 for a resident and for a resident landowner. The dedicated page repeats that price and the notes. The permit runs from the purchase date through the last day of February. It lets Missouri and Arkansas residents fish the other state's portion of the impounded waters of Bull Shoals, Norfork, and Table Rock without a nonresident fishing permit, except trout, if they hold a home-state fishing permit or an exemption. It is an angler reciprocity permit. It is not a guide credential.

The Table Rock Lake special-regulation page states lakewide limits: stop snagging after a daily limit of 2 paddlefish; catfish (channel, blue, and flathead) 10 combined; crappie 15 with a 10-inch minimum; black bass 15-inch minimum; walleye and sauger 18-inch minimum. Paddlefish on the lake and its tributaries have a 34-inch minimum, measured from the eye to the fork of the tail. The page names Barry, Christian, and Greene counties. It is a creel page. It is not a guide roster. The footer on that page is Copyright ©2026 Conservation Commission of Missouri, all rights reserved.

## Guide credential

Current 3 CSR 10-10, downloaded from the Secretary of State current-code PDF, still prints this title: 3 CSR 10-10.770 Guide's Permit: Privileges and Requirements (Rescinded December 31, 1976). The compilation header in that file is November 30, 2025. Chapter 10 does not contain a current sport-fishing guide license. The statewide count of sport-fishing guides is unknown.

The Commercial Fishing Permit is a different credential. 3 CSR 10-10.720 authorizes taking and possessing commercial fish from commercial waters and selling them under 3 CSR 10-10.725. Gear tags are not transferable. Tagged equipment is attended by the permittee or by a licensed commercial fisher named in a writing the assistant carries. The controlling prices are in 3 CSR 10-10.950, whose original rule was filed May 16, 2025, and effective January 1, 2026. That rule says its fees control when they conflict with a fee elsewhere in the chapter. Resident Commercial Fishing Permit $44. Nonresident Commercial Fishing Permit $349. Gear tags: trammel net, gill net, or seine of 100 yards or less, $52 each; each hoop net $9; each 50 hooks or fraction $9. 3 CSR 10-10.771 says commercial permits and replacements are issued only through the department office in Jefferson City, on application and payment. That rule was amended by a filing on May 16, 2025, effective January 1, 2026. No public holder list, bulk file, or issued-count page was opened.

3 CSR 10-11.110, in the chapter whose compilation header is July 31, 2026, prohibits guiding for pay on department areas and says a special-use permit cannot authorize it. 3 CSR 10-11.111 defines commercial use as activity that directly or indirectly produces financial benefit or gain, or where money is exchanged, and it allows some commercial use of department areas under a commercial-use permit or a special-use permit. Annual commercial-use permits run July 1 through June 30. The opened pages do not identify Table Rock Lake or Table Rock State Park as a department area. The department-area ban is a department-area rule.

State Park Marina is supported as the concession at Table Rock State Park. The marina's own site describes boat rentals and slip leases on Table Rock Lake, and the footer on the home page is ©2026 Starboard Marinas. The about page says Starboard Marinas manages the marina. The Missouri State Parks marina page describes a 600-slip concession and names State Park Marina. The parks boating page says the park ramp is near the picnic shelter and the park office, that the ramp has no fee, and that State Park Marina rents watercraft and offers catamaran excursions and fishing guides.

Rental names on the marina site are classes. They include a Ranger fish-boat model, pontoon lengths and passenger counts, and ski boats. The state parks marina page calls one catamaran both 48 feet and 50 feet. Those figures were not joined to a hull identification number or an official number.

The fishing-guide offer is held. Missouri State Parks is the only opened page that says the marina offers fishing guides. The marina home page, rental page, and about page do not list a guide product, a price, a roster, or a captain. The services index linked from the home page was not opened.

Big Cedar Lodge's guided-fishing page is a booking desk. It sells guided bass fishing on Table Rock Lake and guided trout fishing on Lake Taneycomo as separate links, from Bent Hook Marina and from Long Creek Marina. Bent Hook's links are marked for overnight guests. The page states one price block: $400 for 4 hours for 1–2 people, and $100 for each additional person. The price includes the boat, gas, the guide, live bait, and fishing equipment. It does not include a fishing license. Reservations are required. The cancellation block charges a fee unless the trip is canceled at least 21 days ahead, charges 50 percent of the trip rate inside 21 days, and charges 100 percent inside 48 hours. Trout trips are on Lake Taneycomo, and the page says guests use their own transportation to the meeting point. The page does not name a captain or a hull. A direct request to the lodge site returned HTTP 403. The same URL was still readable through a second fetch, which is the copy these figures come from. The checkout links on that page are the desk's own booking flow. They were not counted as a second publisher. Both products are held.

The lodge domain also hosts a hospitality privacy PDF that names Big Cedar Lodge among the sites it covers. That PDF has no revision date on the pages read. It is a visitor-data statement. It refers to community guidelines and terms of use. The paths `/terms/` and `/privacy-policy/` on the lodge site returned HTTP 403, so that content license was not read.

Section 252.228 makes address, telephone number, personal identifying characteristics, date of birth, and unique identification numbers from individual hunting, fishing, and trapping permit records available to the listed public recipients and to the holder. The same fields are inaccessible to other persons after a written request from the holder. The opened text does not create a sport-guide class, and it does not authorize a commercial directory.

The highest-priority permission question is whether any Missouri office will release a current list of businesses that guide sport fishing on Table Rock Lake, limited to business name, water, and any applicable commercial-use authorization, and whether it will authorize commercial-directory reuse. The pages opened on 2026-11-25 do not answer that question. There is no current sport-guide license roll to extract.

## Where to start

Read 3 CSR 10-10.770 for the rescinded guide permit, 3 CSR 10-10.950 for commercial-fishing prices, and 3 CSR 10-11.110 for the department-area rule on guiding for pay. Read the MDC terms and the privacy page before treating any MDC page as a source that can be republished. For Table Rock trips, separate the State Park Marina rental classes, the state parks sentence about fishing guides, and the Big Cedar bass and Taneycomo products. Add a hull only when a public record names that hull.
