# Arizona fishing-source inventory

Checked 2026-11-25. This note records roster counts, permission terms, and the shape of a Lake Powell booking sample. Candidate rows stay out of the repository.

## Two credentials

An Arizona Game and Fish Department guide license is a person credential. A guide, under A.R.S. § 17-101 and R12-4-208, advertises guiding services, is presented to the public for hire, is employed by a commercial enterprise as a guide, or accepts compensation at market value for aiding a person in the field to locate and take wildlife. A landowner or lessee who allows access without full fair-market compensation and advises take is excluded. The license types are hunting (wildlife other than aquatic wildlife), fishing (aquatic wildlife only), and a combination. The license expires December 31. The applicant must be 18 or older and must already hold the matching Arizona hunting, fishing, or combination license. Residency is waived. The fee in the Secretary of State fee table is $300 for residents and $300 for nonresidents. The department's indexed application page says the same $300 payment. A passing score is 80 percent: 50 questions for hunting or fishing, 100 for the combination. A renewal application and a guide report are due by January 10. The report is required even when nobody was guided. A department-sanctioned continuing-education course is required at least once every five years. Arizona has no separate guide board. This license is not a boat list and it is not a Lake Powell list.

Guided fishing on Lake Powell inside Glen Canyon National Recreation Area also needs a National Park Service commercial use authorization for Guided Fishing Service - Lake Powell. That type is separate from Guided Fishing Service - Colorado River. The Lake Powell service is escorting clients by boat on Lake Powell from above Glen Canyon Dam to Hite, with the guide continuously present. Park conditions require an Arizona and/or Utah guide license, a Coast Guard license when clients are carried, and state registration and insurance for the vessel. A Glen Canyon authorization does not by itself authorize business on Navajo Nation trust land. Antelope Point operations have an additional Navajo hiring-preference and business-registration condition. A booking page, a state guide license, a commercial use authorization, and a hull are different records. A brand or a captain name was not treated as hull identity.

## Glen Canyon commercial use

The commercial-use explainer was last updated 2026-11-10:

https://www.nps.gov/glca/getinvolved/cua.htm

A commercial use authorization is required for compensated services that use park resources. Authorizations expire December 31 of the issuance year. Applications are accepted year-round and should be complete at least 45 days before the first trip. The explainer says 2027 applications open on a rolling basis at https://cua.nps.gov/ while 2027 conditions are updated. It states an application fee of $350 for the first service type and $250 for each additional service type in the same season. Fees are non-refundable and do not transfer to the wrong service type. A 2027 authorization is suspended if the 2026 annual report is missing. The explainer points annual reports to the end of February.

https://nps-cua-portal.powerappsportals.us/opportunities/by-park/Terms-Conditions/?id=b6e41d4c-6198-ef11-8a6b-001dd804eef0

The conditions page states an application fee of $350, with a $100 discount if a previous application fee for this park was paid in the same season. That sentence is not the same as the explainer's $250 charge for an additional service type. Both sentences are recorded. Neither was treated as controlling. The management fee on the conditions page is a tiered share of park-based gross receipts: 3 percent under $250,000, 4 percent from $250,000 to $500,000, and 5 percent above $500,000, with the application fee subtracted in the worked example. The annual report is NPS Form 10-660, due February 28. Minimum commercial general liability insurance is $500,000 per occurrence, with the United States as an additional insured. Protection-and-indemnity vessel insurance of $500,000 per occurrence is allowed as an alternative for a vessel. These conditions do not license reuse of operator websites.

Fishing under that authorization is prohibited in closed areas, Rainbow Bridge National Monument, at Glen Canyon Dam, at or through Lone Rock Beach, in the Colorado River corridor, at the Antelope Point private/business ramp, from a harbor shoreline within 200 feet of a floating structure, and inside designated harbors. The harbor exception is fishing from vessels in concession rental slips or on mooring buoys, and from the designated public-use fishing docks at Wahweap Marina and Antelope Point Marina. A dock exception is not a finding that the launch ramp is open.
