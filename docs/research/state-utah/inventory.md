# Utah fishing-source inventory

Checked 2026-11-25. This note records registration rules, permission terms, and the shape of a Flaming Gorge and Green River sample. Candidate rows stay out of the repository.

## Three permissions

A Division of Wildlife Resources certificate of registration is a person or business credential for guiding or outfitting protected wildlife on public land. It is not a boat list and it is not a Flaming Gorge list. The guides page, last updated 2026-05-04, says the 2025 legislature moved this credential from the Division of Professional Licensing to the Department of Natural Resources, effective 2025-07-01. A guide or outfitter compensated more than $100 to assist hunting or fishing on public land must hold the certificate. Applications for 2026 are open. The certificate runs from April 1 through March 31 and must be renewed by March 31. The applicant must finish an orientation course and hold a Utah hunting, fishing, or combination license. An entity outfitter must designate at least one person who holds the matching license. The certificate must be carried in the field. A guide uses an outfitter's affiliate code. The page says an outfitter may guide, and a guide may not outfit.

https://wildlife.utah.gov/guides

The application page is a form, not a name search. It repeats the April 1 through March 31 term and the March 31 filing date.

https://wildlife.utah.gov/guides/application

Rule R657-72, last changed 2026-06-23, restates the guide year and the application contents. An outfitter application must include a service description, the matching license, federal permits needed to operate on federal land, and an attestation that workers' compensation and commercial liability insurance have been obtained. The rule extract does not state a dollar minimum for that insurance. The rules page prints the authorizing citations as 23A-1-101, 23A-4-1202, 23A-4-1203, and 12A-4-1204. That last citation is recorded as printed.

https://wildlife.utah.gov/rules/r657-72

The fee dollars on the guides page are: resident outfitter, all species, $500; resident outfitter, small game, upland, or fishing only, $350; resident guide, all species, $250; resident guide, fishing only, $175; resident spotter, $125. Nonresident amounts on the same page are $1,500, $1,000, $750, $500, and $375 for those same five labels. Utah Code section 23A-4-1202, effective 2026-05-06, says the annual fee is set under section 63J-1-504 and does not print those dollars. The guides page was updated two days before that effective date. The two fee texts were not merged.

https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title23A/Chapter4/C23A-4-S1202_2026050620260506.pdf

Section 23A-4-1201, effective 2026-05-06, defines compensation as economic value over $100. It defines hunting to include fishing. Guide services are guiding, leading, or assisting an individual in hunting protected wildlife. The same code part's index is titled Guide and Outfitter. The guides page, updated 2026-05-04, still defines a spotter and lists spotter fees. The 2026 big-game field regulations say the 2026 legislature removed several spotter requirements. Those sentences were not merged.

https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title23A/Chapter4/23A-4-S1201.html

https://wildlife.utah.gov/guidebooks/field_regs.pdf

Section 23A-4-1202 says the division shall maintain a record of each registered person. No public lookup or download of that record was found. The guides page says an outfitter may email the division for a list of that outfitter's own affiliated guides, and that online management of those names is hoped for later. That email path is not a public roster. No request was submitted. The current public fishing-guide denominator is null.

An angler fishing license is a separate document. The certificate requires it. A Flaming Gorge reciprocal permit lets a resident angler fish the other state's portion of the reservoir. It is not a guide credential.

https://wildlife.utah.gov/flaminggorge

A Forest Service outfitter-and-guide permit is a third permission when the trip uses National Forest System land. The Ashley National Forest permits page, updated 2026-11-23, lists outfitter and guide permits as a category. It does not render a holder list. A 2025 visitor guide points readers to an older forest URL for a guide list. That list was not found as a current file. The Forest Service name count is null.

## Green River and the reservoir

The 2026 fishing guidebook, file posted 2025-11-04, separates the reservoir from the tailwater. From the Colorado line in Browns Park upstream to Flaming Gorge Dam, including Gorge Creek at Little Hole: the trout limit is 3, of which 2 must be under 15 inches and 1 must be over 22 inches; trout from 15 to 22 inches must be released; only artificial flies and lures may be used; fishing from a boat with a motor is closed. That motor closure is a fishing rule. It is not a statement that the reservoir is closed to motors.

https://wildlife.utah.gov/guidebooks/fishing_guidebook.pdf

The Forest Service Green River camps page, updated 2026-11-02, calls section A the seven miles from Flaming Gorge Dam to the Little Hole take-out, with class I and II rapids, and a trail along that stretch. Section B runs from Little Hole toward Browns Park. The Little Hole boat-launch page, updated 2025-03-31, places that ramp east of Dutch John on Forest Road 075. A river-camp page names the Spillway boat ramp as the upper launch below the dam. The 2025 visitor guide lists a boat ramp, restrooms, and a trailhead at the spillway, about one mile below the dam, and boat ramps at Little Hole, about seven river miles below the dam.

https://www.fs.usda.gov/r04/ashley/recreation/green-river-camps

The water-activities page says 26 miles of the Green River below the dam are open to approved non-motorized watercraft without a permit. That sentence is about private river running. It does not replace an outfitter permit for a paid guide. The same page says reservoir boat ramps are a different set of sites.

https://www.fs.usda.gov/r04/ashley/recreation/opportunities/water-activities

On the recreation-area pass page, Cedar Springs Marina is listed at Dutch John, Utah, and Lucerne Valley Marina is listed at Manila, Utah. Buckboard Marina and Firehole are listed on the Wyoming side. Those are facilities and pass vendors. They are not a counted guide roster. Street addresses and telephone numbers on that page were not copied. A reservoir ramp is not the tailwater departure. A motorboat used on the reservoir was not joined to a drift boat used below the dam.

No public certificate list and no Forest Service holder list were available to sample. Five first-party booking pages were opened for the Green River below the dam or for Flaming Gorge in Utah. One more reservoir page was opened and left out so the sample stayed at five. Reseller pages were not used as proof of a hull or a ramp. A local tourism directory that lists guide telephone numbers was not used as a roster.

- Drift-boat and walk-and-wade trips were offered without naming a hull. A statement that a business has two boats did not name either boat.
- One page states a shuttle from the dam to Little Hole. That shuttle is not a named hull, and it was not treated as the departure of every guided trip.
- One page says guides choose the river section on the morning of the trip. A seven-mile description is not a dated departure from Spillway.
- One page prices section A, section B or C, and a separate lake trip. The lake trip was not joined to the river boat.
- One page cites a Forest Service permit number and a Bureau of Land Management permit number. Those numbers were not found on a public holder list. A Wyoming reservoir trip on the same site was not joined to the Utah tailwater.
- One page names a Utah marina as the permit holder for reservoir trips. The marina name was not treated as the boat, and a Lake Powell offer on the same site was not joined to it.
- Two sampled sites reserve all rights. The other sampled sites show a copyright year or no reuse sentence. Silence is unknown, not permission.
- A business city was not treated as a ramp.

## Unresolved

The guides page prints dollar fees. The statute effective 2026-05-06 points those fees to section 63J-1-504 and does not print the dollars. The spotter fee is still on the May 4 page, while the May 6 code extracts that were read do not define a spotter. No public certificate list was found, although the statute says the division keeps a record. The Forest Service permits page names outfitter and guide permits and does not list holders. None of the five booking pages names a hull. No state vessel search was opened, so it is unknown whether an unpowered drift boat appears there. The most useful next primary source is the Forest Service outfitter-and-guide holder list, if the forest publishes one, rather than another operator page.
