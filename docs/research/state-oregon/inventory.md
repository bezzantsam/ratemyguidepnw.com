# Oregon fishing-directory inventory

Checked 2026-11-25. Phase `oregon-inventory`. The landing in this pass is a Depoe Bay booking fleet, read for hull continuity past the guide profiles already in the directory. This note is the public method: which credentials exist, what each publisher allows a commercial directory to do, and the aggregate result of a five-row vessel sample. Business names, guide names, hull names, phone numbers, email addresses, and street addresses stay outside this repository.

No one was contacted. This file is not a listing and not a publishing decision.

## What is registered

The saved lead is the Oregon State Marine Board outfitter-guide and charter page. It describes two person-and-vessel systems.

An outfitter guide is a person who provides outdoor recreational activities for compensation on land or water the person does not own or control. The 2026 fees stated on the page are $350 for an outfitter guide and $500 for a charter guide. On the page, a charter guide is an outfitter guide who holds a U.S. Coast Guard operator license and works on navigable water. Nonresident guide fees are the same as resident fees. A crew-member registration begins in 2026 at $50. Employees who lead or assist angling, other than crew members, must hold their own outfitter-guide registration. The page says a motorized boat on federally navigable water requires a Coast Guard merchant mariner credential. A motorized boat used only on non-navigable water can use that credential or the board's motorized passenger boat operator certification. Those are person credentials. They are not a hull number.

ORS 704.010, opened the same day, is narrower on the charter-guide person. A charter guide is an outfitter and guide who has a Coast Guard operator license and carries no more than six passengers for hire on federally navigable water. An outfitter and guide is a person who, for compensation, provides or advertises outfitting and guiding in this state, or who holds a federal special-use permit for those services. The definition excludes work only on property the person owns or controls. It also excludes an employee, unless that employee conducts angling or operates a boat used for angling. A crew member is an employee who assists angling or boat operation aboard a boat, under the direct supervision of a charter guide or an outfitter and guide who is present, and who is registered with the board. The definition section's history line cites Oregon Laws 2025, chapter 350, section 1.

A charter vessel, on the program page, is a vessel inspected by the Coast Guard that carries 7 or more passengers for hire besides the crew, in the ocean or on inland water, with a Coast Guard-licensed captain. The page says a 6-pack, also called an uninspected passenger vessel, is registered under an outfitter guide as a guide vessel rather than as a charter vessel. Charter vessel registration fees on the page are $50 a year for an Oregon-registered boat and $100 a year for a documented vessel. Nonresident vessel fees are referred to the charter application. That application was not opened.

OAR chapter 250, division 15, is titled Ocean Charter Vessels. Rule 250-015-0002 says the division applies to charter boats carrying passengers for hire on waters of this state. Rule 250-015-0004, amended effective October 6, 2022, is titled Optional Charter Licensing for Uninspected Passenger Vessels. It says a boat carrying fewer than seven passengers for hire for angling, sightseeing, or other recreational purposes may be issued a charter boat license if the owner completes the charter boat application and satisfies rule 250-015-0035(5). Subsection (5) of that sole-state-waters rule, as opened, requires a pre-departure announcement of emergency exits, life-jacket location, and when passengers must wear life jackets. It is not a second passenger-count test. Rule 250-015-0005 says the issued Oregon charter boat license identifies the applicant, the boat, whether the boat operates on sole state waters or within 20 or fewer miles from shore, and the expiration, and that the license is carried on board. The rule does not say the license prints a vessel name.

The public guide search is the ArcGIS experience titled Oregon Guides, Outfitters, and Charters. The item record, modified March 13, 2026, names the roster generator as its source. The item says access constraints are none and the data are considered public domain. It also says the data are as-is, must not be used as a legal document, should cite the Marine Board, and that modifications should be described. A person count was not taken from the dashboard on this date. The activity-specific guide downloads on the roster generator were not downloaded. They are person lists. A contact name, telephone, and email on the item record were not copied.

The "Online Lookup" link on the guide page opens Boat Oregon Store report 340. That page offers three public-record searches: boat, contact, and floating property. The boat form searches vessel ID, hull identification number, model year, hull material, length, and manufacturer. It has no vessel-name field and no export control. No boat query was submitted. A blank or manufacturer-wide query would return owner records, and those fields were not collected.

## Charter boat download

The roster generator at the Boat Oregon Store says the lists below are available for public use, and that a list not shown can be requested from the Marine Board. Active Charter Boats is marked no fee required. The download on 2026-11-25 is a CSV. Its columns are charter boat license number, credential status, expiration date, contact name, phone, email, mailing address, city, state, ZIP, charter boat ID, hailing port, distance from port, services, and passenger count excluding crew. The contact columns were not copied, and the file was deleted after the counts were checked.

The file has 132 data rows and 70 unique license numbers. Each unique license has one charter boat ID. Extra rows repeat a license across services. The service combinations are mostly fishing plus scenic tours. Status values on the unique licenses were not retabulated after the row-level count: the row file contains 92 rows marked active in renewal, 39 marked active, and 1 pending. Sixty-nine of the 70 licenses have an expiration value. Those values run from 20261231 through 20281231.

Hailing port is filled on 69 of the 70 licenses. Depoe Bay is the hailing port on 11 unique licenses. Of those 11, 7 are active in renewal and 4 are active. Seven expire in 2026 and four expire in 2027. Seven are marked more than 20 miles from port and four less than 20 miles. Passenger counts excluding crew on those 11 are four boats at 42 and one each at 16, 27, 29, 33, 38, 43, and 45. The file has no vessel-name column. A charter boat ID is a short identifier. Five of the 70 unique IDs use an OR prefix and 10 are all digits. That ID was not treated as a hull name.

Two unique licenses in the statewide file have a passenger count of 6. None of the 11 Depoe Bay licenses is in that pair. Those 11 list 16, 27, 29, 33, 38, 42, 43, or 45 passengers. The program page describes the inspected charter vessel as 7 or more passengers besides the crew, and it places a 6-pack under an outfitter guide. ORS 704.010 defines the charter-guide person as someone who carries no more than six. Rule 250-015-0004 separately allows a charter boat license for a boat carrying fewer than seven. The two six-passenger rows fit that optional license. They were not matched to a vessel name, because the file has none.

The 11 Depoe Bay licenses were not sampled as named hulls. There is no name on the row to join to a booking page.

The same booking desk is already a species profile in the project directory. Those profiles do not carry a hull number. A prior attribute points at one section of this fleet page for a fishing method. That attribute is a guide profile. It was not joined to a hull.

The highest-priority permission question is whether "public use" of the charter boat file, plus the dashboard's public-domain sentence, allows a commercial directory to republish a license number and a boat ID after the contact columns are removed. The most useful next primary source is the charter vessel application, which is the form that would show whether a vessel name is collected and then left off the public file. A fleet page that prints a length does not authorize a directory to claim that the hull is a specific charter boat license.

## Where to start

Read an outfitter guide as a person registration. Read a charter guide, under ORS 704.010, as that person with a Coast Guard operator license who carries no more than six passengers on federally navigable water. Read the program page's charter vessel as the inspected boat that carries 7 or more passengers besides the crew. Read a boat that carries fewer than seven as eligible for an optional charter boat license under OAR 250-015-0004, and also as the guide-vessel case the program page describes. Add a Depoe Bay hull only when a dated public row and a booking page print the same charter boat ID, documentation number, or hull identification number.
