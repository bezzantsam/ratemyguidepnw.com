# Montana fishing-directory sources and reuse

Checked 2026-11-25. Scope is the Missouri River near Craig and the Madison Valley. This note records how the credentials differ, what each publisher lets a commercial directory do, and the aggregate result of one defined sample. It does not name people, businesses, license numbers, or permit-customer records. Private evidence stays outside this repository.

No one was contacted. Nothing here is a listing, a claim, or a publishing decision.

## Four instruments, kept apart

A paid fishing trip in this sample can touch four different instruments. A booking page does not establish any of them. A shop name does not establish them either.

**Outfitter license (a person).** Montana Code Annotated 2025, 37-47-101, defines an outfitter as a person who, for consideration, provides equipment, watercraft, or personal service to pursue game, including fish, and who either accompanies the participant or supervises a licensed guide or an outfitter's assistant. Section 37-47-304 says the application is in the name of an individual person. A business entity applies through one qualifying person, and the license is issued in that person's name. Section 37-47-310 says the license may not be transferred. An operations plan may say the license is held for the use and benefit of a named business. The business is still not the licensee. Section 37-47-301 requires a license before a person acts as, or advertises as, an outfitter or guide, and forbids an outfitter to hire a guide who lacks a current license.

**Guide license (a different person).** Section 37-47-101 defines a guide as a person employed by, or contracting with, a licensed outfitter. Section 37-47-404(3) says a guide may not offer services, and a customer may not use those services, unless they are obtained through an endorsing outfitter. An outfitter's assistant is an emergency substitute and may not represent himself or herself as an outfitter or guide. A label on a booking page, including a Coast Guard captain title, is not a Montana guide license.

**River and site use (a permit, not a license).** Fish, Wildlife and Parks requires a commercial-use permit to conduct commercial use on fishing access sites, wildlife management areas, some rivers, and state parks. Guided angling is inside that commercial-use definition. On the Madison River, FWP and the Bureau of Land Management have required a special recreation permit since 2008. The 2026 Madison application says the permit holder must be a licensed outfitter when the use is hunting or angling, and it asks for a copy of the 2026 Board license when the use is fishing. The fee printed on the form is 3 percent of that year's unadjusted gross Madison revenue, with a $130 minimum paid with the application and the rest due by 2027-01-10. The same form prints a return date of 2025-04-01 and a case-by-case date of 2026-04-01. Both dates are on the form. Applicants are not authorized until a signed permit and boat tags come back. The tags are two per set, detachable, and displayed on both sides. They are permit tags. They are not a hull registry.

**The water and the access site.** Craig Fishing Access Site is a Region 4 site on the Missouri River with a concrete ramp, open year-round. Ennis Fishing Access Site is a Region 3 site on the Madison River with a gravel ramp, open April 27 through November 30, and day use only from December 1 through April 1. Neither site is an operator. Neither was treated as a departure for a booked trip. The Region 3 access-site index is paginated, and its full count was not taken.

Board watercraft decals are ordered inside the authenticated licensee portal. The public watercraft page does not list hull names. A description of drift-boat fishing is not a vessel.

## What was available, and what was not

The Board's license lookup is the public portal linked from the individual-lookup page. On this date the interactive page presented a verification challenge and was not completed. An automated request was rejected. No licensee row was read. The portal help page is a navigation stub and does not define fields. There is no public field dictionary beyond the lookup disclaimer and the form labels.

The department offers an emailed, customizable licensee list and says phone numbers are omitted. The request form was not submitted, and no list was downloaded. The full roster count is unknown and is left null.

The Board's regulations page says the web text of the administrative rules may differ from the official Secretary of State text. A stable official HTML page for the current watercraft-tag rule was not retrieved. The Board's combined statute-and-rule PDF is dated as of 2023-06-30 and is labeled so that the Secretary of State text prevails.

Client records kept by an outfitter were not requested. The department's records page says pending complaints are confidential. This check did not open a complaint file.

No issued count of Madison special recreation permits, commercial-use permits, or fishing access sites was posted on the pages that were read.

## Sample

The sample is four rows from two bookable operations, one on the Missouri near Craig and one in the Madison Valley. Several pages on one shop are one publisher. A second outfitter license number printed on the Missouri booking page was left out of the sample: the page spells the surname two ways, and no second publisher was opened for it.

One row is supported. Three are held. Sampled rows equal supported plus held (4 = 1 + 3). The supported row is a published outfitter identity that a Department of Commerce tourism record repeats, including the license number. The Board portal was not completed, so that row is a published match and not a confirmed current license. The same booking page says that outfitter is no longer guiding. The page is undated. The sentence was kept as a role statement, which fits an outfitter who supervises guides, and it was not treated as a Board status. It has no separate source date for a crew assignment.

The Madison operation is held. An association membership page states a license number and says the details come from the membership directory, not from the Board. The operator's trips URL returned only a title. Indexed text of that site describes bookable float and wade trips and uses a second business name that does not carry the license number. Membership is not a state license.

The guide row is held. Staff labels on the Missouri booking page do not include a Montana guide license number. No person is named in this report. No historical crew list with a source date was found.

The vessel row is held. The booking page describes drift-boat fishing and transportation to the river. It names no hull, documentation number, or owner of a boat. No hull was inferred from a brand. One independent publisher match is the Commerce record behind the supported outfitter identity. No second publisher matched a hull, a guide license, a fishing access site departure, or a Madison permit.

A current booking link, a commercial-reuse grant, and authority to claim a listing are three different things. This sample has a booking link and one published identity match. It has neither of the other two.

## Reuse

Montana Code Annotated 2025, 1-11-304, says the code and its ancillary publications may not be copyrighted. That covers the statute text cited here. It does not cover license-lookup records or Fish, Wildlife and Parks page design.

Section 2-6-1003 gives every person a right to examine and copy public information, with safety, donor, and wildlife-location exceptions. That right is not a commercial-directory grant.

Section 2-6-1017 generally bars an agency from distributing or selling a distribution list of personal contact information without permission, and bars use of a person-list as a distribution list without permission. Subsection (4)(d) excepts professional and occupational license lists under Title 37, chapter 47, which is the outfitter chapter. The exception removes those lists from that ban. It does not authorize copying a roster into a commercial fishing directory, and it does not authorize a listing claim. The department's own mailing-list page still withholds phone numbers. This check did not take the list.

The Board lookup disclaimer is silent on commercial reuse, attribution, bulk access, and a permission process. The state internet privacy and security policy, linked from the Board site, is a privacy policy. It limits disclosure of personal information collected from website users, and it limits sale of constituent email for marketing, unless the person opts in. It does not grant directory reuse of license records. Its change process is an internal information-technology request, not a records desk.

Fish, Wildlife and Parks terms of use, section 4, let anyone view, copy, or distribute information on the pages, excluding design and layout, for personal or informational use, if the documents are not modified, unless a particular item restricts that use. Section 2 does not warrant that the materials are free of copyright. The terms state no attribution line beyond leaving the documents unmodified, no bulk feed, and no desk for permission to republish the pages in a commercial directory. A permit to conduct commercial use on state land is a different question from a license to republish the website.

The association page and the operator pages are silent on commercial directory reuse. That silence is unknown, not a permission. No marketing copy is repeated here.

No source checked here grants bulk commercial reuse of a named license or permit list.

## Unresolved

The first permission question is whether Labor and Industry and Fish, Wildlife and Parks will each authorize the fields and the commercial directory use before any license status or access-site fact is republished. The occupational-list exception and the personal-or-informational copying clause do not answer that question.

Also unresolved: the number of current outfitter licenses, guide licenses, Madison special recreation permits, and fishing access sites. The live status of every license number seen in the private sample. Which of the two printed Madison application dates controls. The official current text of the watercraft-tag rule. No hull in the sample was tied to a name, a documentation number, or an owner.
