# Hawaii fishing-directory sources and reuse

Checked 2026-11-25. Scope is Kona and Honokōhau Small Boat Harbor. This note records how the credentials differ, what each publisher lets a commercial directory do, and the aggregate result of one fleet sample. It does not list vessels, people, license numbers, or permit-customer records. Private evidence stays outside this repository.

No one was contacted. Nothing here is a listing, a claim, or a publishing decision.

## Three credentials, kept apart

A Kona passenger fishing boat can touch three different state instruments. A booking page does not establish any of them. A berth does not establish the other two.

**Commercial marine license (person).** HRS 189-2 requires a person who takes marine life for commercial purposes to hold a commercial marine license. The same section requires any person providing charter services for the taking of marine life to hold that license. HAR 13-74-20 repeats both duties. The Division of Aquatic Resources program page states the same charter inclusion. Fees posted there are $100 for a resident and $250 for a non-resident. The license holder is a person. The license is not a hull and not a harbor slip.

**Commercial marine vessel license (optional, one vessel).** HRS 189-2 makes a vessel used for commercial take eligible for a commercial marine vessel license, and says one valid vessel license satisfies the person-license requirement for everyone taking marine life commercially aboard that vessel. HAR 13-74-23, the agency FAQ updated 2025-04-14, and the April 2025 application agree on the shape of that license: it is issued to a commercial marine licensee for one vessel, it expires with that person's license, it is not transferable, and the licensee need not be aboard. The posted chapter compilation was adopted on 2024-01-12 and is marked an unofficial copy. For a charter, HAR 13-74-23(g) and the FAQ say the vessel license covers crew and does not cover passengers. Nonresident customers still need the nonresident recreational marine fishing license. The vessel license also does not itself authorize sale of the catch. Only a person with a commercial marine license may sell it.

**Harbor commercial use, separate from the fishing licenses.** DOBOR's commercial-operation FAQ says operators of commercial vessels need a commercial use permit for state ocean waters under HAR 13-256-3, including operators who already hold a harbor permit. The 2014 amendment that was read says the ocean-waters application duty has an exception for a state commercial harbor or Kewalo Basin, and it points applicants back to HAR 13-231-50 through 13-231-70. Those exceptions are not Honokōhau. Honokōhau is a DOBOR small boat harbor.

Inside the harbor, HAR 13-231-57 is the passenger rule. It covers commercial vessels, including commercial fishing vessels engaged in charter fishing, unless the vessel's total income comes from sale of fish or permitted coral. A sportfishing passenger operation does not fit that sale-of-fish exemption on the text of the rule. Loading or discharging passengers at a small boat harbor requires both a regular mooring permit and a valid commercial use permit, or a catamaran registration certificate. A mooring without the commercial permit is not authority to embark passengers. A fishing license is not a slip.

The blank forms match that split. The commercial use permit application and the harbor mooring application are different PDFs, filed with the district or harbor office. Some require an original signature. No filled roster is posted.

## What was available, and what was not

The commercial marine license portal is an application and renewal system. It is not a public fleet list. Renewal of a vessel license asks the holder for a record the holder already has. The Online Dealer Reports guide describes a public license search for a dealer who needs to confirm that a fisher selling catch holds a valid commercial marine license. The guide's result fields are license number, licensee name, vessel name, license type, status, expiration date, and business name. A non-script request of that search on this date returned no rows. No name was queried. The guide does not offer a download, an API, or a count.

HRS 189-3 makes catch-report submissions confidential, with narrow exceptions, and allows the department to publish aggregate figures that do not identify the submitter. The commercial fishing page repeats that limit. The 2025 Commercial Marine Landings Summary Trend Report is pounds and value. It is not a licensee or vessel list. The official HRS 189-2 page notes Attorney General Opinion 85-23: public disclosure of commercial marine licenses is not permitted, though disclosure of some information is allowed. A validation lookup is not a directory grant.

Honokōhau's facility page lists 262 moorings, three ramps, and the piers. It names no charter. HAR 13-231-58(d) caps commercial use permits for vessels assigned a permanent mooring at Honokōhau at 120. HAR 13-231-67 caps launch-ramp commercial use permits there at 35. Both numbers are maximums in an unofficial rule compilation. Neither is a count of permits currently issued, and the ramp cap is not the moored charter fleet. Issued counts were not found. The full charter denominator is unknown and is left null.

An open-data catalog query for a commercial marine license dataset did not return a dataset page, so no open-data roster was verified.

## Sample

One directly bookable operator site was the fleet sample. Multiple pages on that site are one publisher. The charters page says it has six charter boats and names six. Another page on the same site says seven boats. The charters page also offers passenger capacity on two boats it does not name. The sample recorded three named offerings and the unnamed passenger offering. The other named blocks were left unread as separate records.

Zero of the four were supported. Four were held. Sampled rows equal supported plus held (4 = 0 + 4). No second publisher independently matched a hull to a mooring, a commercial use permit, a commercial marine license, or a commercial marine vessel license. One named offering states a Honokōhau departure on the operator page only. One name is attached to two incompatible hull descriptions on the same site. One named block conflicts with another length claim on the same page and does not state the harbor. The unnamed offering cannot be tied to a hull, so none was inferred. The operator directions page is an image and was not used as a departure fact.

A current booking link, a commercial-reuse grant, and authority to claim a listing are three different things. This sample has the first only, and only as an operator claim.

## Reuse

Hawaii.gov Terms of Use, read at the URL below, cover official sites whose addresses contain `hawaii.gov` or `eHawaii.gov`. They prohibit commercial use or resale of data from those services without a separate written agreement with the State. They prohibit automated access. They prohibit duplication of content for commercial purposes or in a way that implies official approval. Permission requests go to the agency that provides the service. The address `terms@ehawaii.gov` is stated not to answer those requests. DAR and DOBOR pages and the licensing portals fall under those terms. The statute pages are public law. The hosted rule PDFs are marked unofficial and still sit on a state site.

The operator site reserves copyright and its privacy policy does not grant directory reuse, bulk access, or an attribution line. That silence is "not granted," not a permission.

No source checked here grants bulk commercial reuse of a named license or permit list.

## Unresolved

The first permission question is whether DAR and DOBOR will each sign a written agreement that names the fields and the directory use before any licensee name, vessel name, license number, or harbor permit record is republished.

Also unresolved: the number of current commercial marine licenses, commercial marine vessel licenses, Honokōhau moorings used by charter vessels, and Honokōhau commercial use permits actually issued. The later compiled text of HAR 13-256 was indexed but not line-checked against the 2014 amendment read here. No hull in the sample was tied to a USCG document, a state HA number, a slip, or a current license.
