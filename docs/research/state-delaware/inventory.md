# Delaware fishing directory inventory

Checked 2026-11-25. Waters in scope: Indian River Inlet and Lewes. This note records the licensing lookup, the reuse terms that were actually printed, and aggregate sample counts. Individual boats, captains, and booking sites are not named here.

No one was contacted. No listing was published.

## What was found

Delaware does not publish a charter, head boat, or fishing guide roster. The credential that can be applied for in public is a vessel license. A charter boat is a vessel-for-hire engaged in recreational fishing and hired per trip. A head boat is a vessel-for-hire engaged in recreational fishing and hired per person. Those definitions are 7 Del. C. § 906(9) and § 906(36). The fees are $150 and $300 for a charter boat and $300 and $600 for a head boat, resident then nonresident. The Division of Fish and Wildlife issues them directly. There is no agent fee on those two rows.

The application, last revised 9/19/2024, is filled out by the vessel owner. It asks for a captain credential, or the primary operator's captain credential if the owner is not a captain, plus a boat registration or documentation number that is valid for commercial passenger use, a boat name, length, passenger capacity, and a home port or area of operation. Only operators listed on the application may use the license. The form is not a directory.

A separate fishing guide fee is $100 resident and $300 nonresident, with a $2.50 agent fee. The fee page defines paid hunting guide service. It does not define a fishing guide. No guide application and no guide roster were found. Fishing licenses, including the charter and head boat rows on the fee page, are calendar-year licenses and expire December 31. A charter or head boat license covers people fishing from that vessel, so those anglers do not need a separate Fisherman Information Network number. When a federal or interstate plan requires monitoring, 7 Del. C. § 941 adds a free reporting permit and a Department log. That permit is not the paid license, and it is not published as a list.

## Reuse

7 Del. C. §§ 503 and 506 say information in hunting, fishing, and trapping licenses and related databases may not be made available to the public. Government agencies may use it for fish and wildlife law enforcement and for Department harvest surveys. Social Security numbers are excluded from the survey use. The Department may give a contractor the same information, still without Social Security numbers, only to retain or recruit hunters, trappers, and anglers and to sustain license sales, and only under a confidentiality agreement. That is not permission for a commercial directory.

The Delaware.gov privacy policy governs visitor information. It says the portal will not sell or lease that information for direct marketing, and that a public record may still be disclosable under the Freedom of Information Act. It does not dedicate agency directories to the public domain. DNREC pages checked this date carry a footer marked ©MMXXVI. A FOIA request to DNREC is written access. It does not, by itself, license republication.

The open-data fishing table is a different object. Its metadata license is public domain. The table has 45 year rows of resident and nonresident fishing-license and trout-stamp totals. The latest year stored in the metadata is 2019. It has no vessel, port, or guide field. The DNREC open-data index does not list a charter, head boat, or fishing guide holder file. The public-domain grant stops at the tables that are actually on the portal.

## Indian River Inlet

Delaware State Parks publishes a referral page for the charter fleet at Indian River Marina in Delaware Seashore State Park. The page has no as-of date and does not say the entries hold a current DNREC license. It parsed to 27 named boat entries. A typical entry has a boat label, a length and model, a captain name, and sometimes a website. One captain name is attached to two different boat labels. A park brochure at a March 2025 path returned HTTP 403, so it could not be used to date the list.

Three of the 27 entries were opened against the booking site linked from the row. Two booking sites agree with the referral page on length and on builder or model. Those two are supported hulls. One booking site does not state length or builder, so that service is held. The second boat label that shares a captain name with a supported row was not described on the booking pages that were opened, and it was not joined. None of the opened pages displayed a DNREC license number or a hull identification number. One booking site states no terms. Two claim all rights reserved. The referral page itself states no reuse terms, so its reuse status is unknown.

## Lewes

No agency page filters licenses by Lewes. One canal landing publishes four named hulls on its own fleet page and claims all rights reserved for 2026. A chamber listing confirms that the business offers headboat fishing and charters and links the fleet page. The chamber listing does not name a hull. One of the four hulls was sampled and stays held, because the length and builder are the operator's own statement. The other three names on that page were not given separate rows.

## Unresolved

The first permission question is whether DNREC will authorize a commercial directory to copy or refresh charter, head boat, or fishing guide records. The statute currently withholds the license databases.

Also unresolved: the meaning of the fishing guide license, whether Digital DNREC has any public holder search, whether the unread marina brochure still matches the 27-entry page, and whether a head boat operates at Indian River Marina without appearing on that page. The short Digital DNREC address was rejected on this check. The permitting host returned a portal title and no catalog.
