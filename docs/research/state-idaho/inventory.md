# Idaho fishing-directory sources and reuse

Checked 2026-11-25. Scope is the Salmon River, chiefly the Middle Fork and the Main Salmon: the statewide outfitter license, the water-specific operating-area caps, the forest permit lists, and one inspected launch. This note records what each publisher states about reuse and the aggregate result of one defined sample. It does not name people, businesses, license numbers, or client records. Private evidence stays outside this repository.

No one was contacted. Nothing here is a listing, a claim, or a publishing decision.

## Records kept apart

A paid Salmon River trip can touch several records. A booking page does not establish any of them.

**Outfitter license.** Idaho Code 36-2104, read from the legislature's site, makes it a misdemeanor to act as an outfitter or guide without a license. Section 36-2102, in the text marked effective until July 1, 2028, defines an outfitter as a person who holds out for hire, provides facilities and services for consideration, and uses equipment or accommodations for compensation for listed activities, including float or power boating and fishing on Idaho rivers. A firm must designate an agent. A guide, in that section, is a natural person employed by a licensed outfitter. The license year in that section runs from April 1 through March 31. Section 36-2108 requires an outfitter application to include a worded description of the operating-area boundaries. The board's fee table lists an outfitter license at $400, a designated agent at $140, and a guide at $105. The same page lists a Clearwater boat sticker at $15. That sticker, in current rule 206, is for Clearwater sections CL2 and CL3. It is not a Salmon River hull number.

**Operating area, which is not the person license.** Current IDAPA 24.35.01 defines an operating area as the area the board assigns. Rule 259 sets power and float maxima by river section. For the Middle Fork, section MF1 runs from Boundary Creek to Cache Bar, with no power maximum and a float maximum of 27. The row does not add a per-outfitter boat sentence. On other Salmon rows, a sentence limits boats per outfitter and the column numbers are different, so a column is not automatically a boat count. Main Salmon sections in the same rule include SA5, North Fork to Corn Creek, and SA6, Corn Creek to the Spring Bar boat ramp. SA6 states no outfitter fishing below Vinegar Creek from September 15 through March 31 unless the Bureau of Land Management permits it and the board's executive officer concurs. These rows limit how many authorizations may exist. They do not name the holders.

The current rule's legal-authority line, dated July 1, 2026, cites Title 39, Chapter 21. The same rule's definition of the Act, and the board's statutes page, cite Title 36, Chapter 21. Both lines were read as printed. A second text of section 36-2102, marked effective July 1, 2028, is on the legislature's page and was not compared.

**Federal permission and the float permit.** The Salmon-Challis National Forest publishes lists of outfitters it has permitted. Those lists are not the state license file. A year-round float permit is required on the Middle Fork from Dagger Falls to the confluence with the Main Salmon. The forest page says Recreation.gov handles reservations, lottery applications, cancellations, and payments, and that a reservation is not the permit. The permit is emailed by the river office. The control-season lottery, May 28 through September 3, is described for private boating permits. The same page caps launches at seven float parties a day, commercial and noncommercial together. It does not publish a commercial launch calendar.

**The boat.** Rule 206 requires words, names, or letters at least three inches high on each float or power boat an outfitter uses. That is an outfitter label, not a hull identification number. The Middle Fork equipment list requires an Idaho invasive-species sticker for each boat, as applicable. The Fish and Game page says that sticker is bought from Idaho Parks and Recreation. The Parks and Recreation boating page links registration renewal to a separate address. That address returned a human-verification challenge and no certificate. Title 67, Chapter 70, the Idaho Safe Boating Act link on that page, was not read. No hull search was used.

**Angler fishing.** The outfitter rule defines fishing by reference to Fish and Game season rules, IDAPA 13.01.11, and the outfitter's operating plan. The angler-license purchase page was not opened.

## Reuse

The board privacy policy, last updated November 1, 2024, says personal information supplied for licensing is exempt from disclosure under the Idaho public-records law. Other information collected on the site becomes a public record that may be inspected and copied unless another exemption applies. If the policy conflicts with that law, the law controls. The policy does not grant commercial-directory reuse, it does not state an attribution line, and it does not describe a bulk feed.

The public-records page cites Idaho Code 74-102 and says a request must be in writing. It points to a request form. Complaints and investigations are exempt under sections 74-106(9) and 74-113(3)(a). The page prints a custodian inbox. No request was sent.

The forest pages link a Department of Agriculture privacy policy. That address returned access denied on this check, so its reuse terms are unknown.

One rendered operator site says that no part may be duplicated without written permission. Another carries a 2024 copyright line and does not add a reuse sentence. The third was silent on reuse. Silence is unknown. The association home page, as fetched, showed no copyright or privacy sentence. No marketing copy is repeated here.

No source checked on this pass grants bulk commercial reuse of a named guide list. The forest page does publish dated name-and-website lists. Nothing read on this pass authorizes copying those lists into a directory.

## Unresolved

The permission question is whether a result from the public license search is a public record that may be reused in a commercial directory. The privacy policy exempts personal information supplied for licensing and allows inspection and copying of other information. It does not grant directory reuse. The search was opened only as a script shell, so the fields on a result were not seen. That search is the most useful next primary source.

Also unresolved: why the current rule's authority line cites Title 39, Chapter 21, while the Act defined in the same rule is Title 36, Chapter 21. Whether commercial Middle Fork launches are assigned outside the private-boater lottery. Which Salmon ramp, if any, is the departure for the held bookings. Boundary Creek was not named on those pages. Whether a motorboat certificate can be obtained with a hull identification number and without a home address. Whether the July 1, 2028 text of section 36-2102 changes who must hold the outfitter license. The board's memorandum link with the Forest Service and the Bureau of Land Management returned HTTP 404, so the written split between a state license and a federal permit was not read there.
