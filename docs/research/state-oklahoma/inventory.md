# Oklahoma fishing-directory inventory

Checked 2026-11-25. Phase `oklahoma-inventory`. The water in this pass is Lake Texoma, on trips that depart from the Oklahoma side. This note records the credential, the reuse terms that were actually opened, and the aggregate result of a three-row sample. People, telephone numbers, email addresses, street addresses, and postal boxes stay outside this repository.

No one was contacted. This file is not a listing and not a publishing decision.

## What is licensed

The starting page is the Oklahoma Department of Wildlife Conservation fishing-guide directory at `https://www.wildlifedepartment.com/fishing/fishing-guides`. It says an annual fishing guide license is required for residents and nonresidents who assist, accompany, transport, guide, or aid persons in the taking of fish for commercial purposes. It calls the table below "known licensed fishing guides."

The table has three columns: business name, contact, and waters guided. The contact column is a telephone link. Those numbers were not copied. The page does not offer a download, a hull field, or a departure city. The footer is Copyright ©2026 Oklahoma Department of Wildlife Conservation, all rights reserved.

The opened table has 348 data rows and 337 unique business-name strings. Ten business-name strings appear more than once, so the row count is not a count of unique businesses. One hundred thirty-seven rows contain the letters "texoma" in the waters field. That figure mixes "Lake Texoma" with longer water lists. It is not a count of guides who depart from Oklahoma. Thirty-three waters fields read as person names rather than water names, and 24 begin with "All." The directory's own columns are not a clean water index.

29 O.S. § 4-113A, as displayed by the Oklahoma State Courts Network with a 2026 citation, requires a license before a person assists, accompanies, transports, guides, or aids another in taking fish for compensation or other consideration. The Department promulgates the rules. The statutory fee may not exceed $200 a year. Landowners and agricultural lessees who provide the service on property they own or lease are outside the section. The guide license does not replace any other fishing license. A conviction is punishable by a $1,000 fine, up to six months in county jail, or both. The historical line says Laws 2009, Senate Bill 277, chapter 268, emergency effective July 1, 2009. The section licenses a person. It does not license a boat.

The current license-fee page prints one Fishing Guide License at $90, valid January 1 through December 31, and says the cost is reduced to $20 with Coast Guard mariner credentials. It does not print a separate nonresident guide price. The same page prices an angler annual fishing license at $31 for residents 18 or older, valid 365 days, and $81 for nonresidents 18 or older. A one-day angler license is $11 resident and $26 nonresident. A Lake Texoma license is $12 for residents and $12 for nonresidents, valid January 1 through December 31. It allows fishing on both the Oklahoma and Texas portions of Lake Texoma and is not valid below the dam. That is an angler reciprocity permit, not a guide credential.

A fishing-guide application PDF still hosted by the department, in a September 2022 file path, states the same $90 and $20 fees for residents and nonresidents and asks whether the applicant wants the department to publish business contact information. It also says a person assisting the licensed guide in the same vessel is exempt, that the guide license does not replace an Oklahoma fishing license, and that the requirement began July 31, 2010. The form collects identity and payment fields. Those fields were not copied. The live directory does not say whether every current holder answered yes to publication. The statewide count of issued guide licenses is unknown. The 348 rows are the published table.

The department's licensing home URL redirects to a page titled Customer Lookup. That page did not present the guide directory.

The Lake Texoma where-to-fish page places the lake in Bryan, Johnston, Love, and Marshall counties, 5 miles east of Kingston on U.S. 70. It gives shoreline 592.70 miles and surface area 88,000 acres. It says Oklahoma and Texas regulations may differ. The Oklahoma limits on that page are: black bass five combined per day, with a 14-inch minimum for largemouth and smallmouth; striped bass and hybrids 10 combined, of which only two may be 20 inches or longer; white bass 25; crappie 37 combined with a 10-inch minimum; flathead catfish five with a 20-inch minimum. Alligator gar harvest is closed May 1 through May 31, and a harvest must be reported within 24 hours. The page points readers to the guide directory. It does not list guides.

Catfish Bay Marina is supported as a landing and as a striper-guide desk. Its own marina page, on the Pointe Vista site, says the marina is on Lake Texoma, that it has more than 300 wet slips from 24 to 65 feet, and that striper guides are available from the gas dock. The page prices guide service at $450 for 1 to 3 people and $150 for each additional person per boat. It says bait and equipment are furnished and the catch is cleaned. It says the guides are certified and licensed in Oklahoma and Texas, that the guest needs a Texoma fishing license, and that the guides are independent and are not employees of the marina or Pointe Vista. The TravelOK page for Lake Texoma State Park calls the marina privately operated, places it inside the park, and says it offers striper guide fishing. A separate TravelOK marina profile repeats that the marina offers striper guides and describes rental classes, including pontoons. Those two TravelOK pages are one publisher. The $450 price, the dual-state license sentence, and the seven-name list were not on the tourism pages. Rental durations and slip lengths are classes. The marina page links an editor address for a guide-association document. That address was not treated as a public roster.

Scarberry's Guide Service is held. The TravelOK profile places it at Catfish Bay Marina and says it runs three fully rigged 23-foot Falcon boats, that the boats meet Coast Guard safety standards, and that the guides are Coast Guard licensed. The park page names the same business among the Texoma Striper Guide Association at the marina. The profile and the park page are the same tourism publisher. The profile does not link an operator website. The business-name column on the ODWC directory did not contain this business string. A 23-foot Falcon description is a class. It is not a hull identification number. The Coast Guard sentence is the profile's claim.

Blue Water Striper Guide Service is held. The TravelOK profile says it fishes from the Oklahoma side at Lake Texoma State Park and Catfish Bay Marina, that it is a member of the Catfish Bay Guide Association, and that a 23-foot Falcon Striper Express seats up to six. The same profile links `http://www.texomastriperguidefishing.com`. That address returned HTTP 404. The park page names the business. The ODWC business-name column did not contain this string. Absence from that column is not a licensure finding, because the directory publishes a "known" list and its waters column is not clean. The two tourism profiles do not use the same postal code. Neither code was copied.

No unique hull was identified. A boat length, a model name, or a skipper was not joined to a physical hull.

## Where to start

Read 29 O.S. § 4-113A, the fee row for the Fishing Guide License, and the disclaimer before treating the guide table as a source that can be republished. The table is a person-and-business list with a waters field. It is not a hull file and it does not show an Oklahoma departure. For Lake Texoma trips, start at Catfish Bay Marina inside Lake Texoma State Park and keep the marina, the $450 guide price, and each named guide separate. Add a hull only when a public record names that hull.
