# Connecticut fishing-source inventory

Checked 2026-11-25. This note records the person guide registration, the party and charter vessel registration, and a five-hull sample at Waterford and Niantic. Candidate rows stay out of the repository.

## Two different credentials

Section 26-31c is a person registration. No person may conduct hunting or fishing guide services in Connecticut without registering with the Department of Energy and Environmental Protection. The fee is $100. The registration is not transferable and expires on the last day of December after it is issued. A person who operates a vessel for those services on navigable waters must also hold a current Coast Guard passenger-for-hire license. That text was read from the current official compilation.

Subsection (b) exempts the user, operator, or crew of a vessel registered as a charter boat, party boat, or head boat under section 26-142a from the registration requirement in subsection (a). The department licenses page describes the same exemption for guide services on the registered vessel. Subsection (c) says a person who provides fishing guide services from a vessel while taking or landing marine species may not have more than two customers aboard. The statute does not say that sentence caps a vessel that already holds the party or charter registration. It was not used as the party-boat capacity rule.

Section 26-142a is the vessel credential that matches a Waterford or Niantic fishing departure. A party or charter fishing vessel is a vessel that carries passengers for hire as a recreational fishing platform to take and land marine regulated species. No person may operate one unless the commissioner has issued a registration for that vessel to its owner. The current compilation sets the fee at $315. The person who operates it must hold a current Coast Guard passenger-for-hire license. A separate registration is required for each vessel. The April 2024 application prints the same $315 fee and asks for vessel name, state registration or Coast Guard number, length, passenger capacity, home port, Connecticut landing port, and marina. Those registration numbers are not on a public list.

Passengers fishing on a party or charter fishing vessel registered under section 26-142a, and operating only in the marine district, do not need a fishing license. The same exemption is in the marine-waters license section. This department registration is not the motor-vehicle vessel number. A boating section also lets a person who holds both the Coast Guard license and this registration operate a marine-dealer-numbered vessel for a recreational charter guide service. That path was not treated as a roster.

Section 26-157b requires each party or charter registration holder to report, on the commissioner's schedule, information that can include how many people were carried to fish, the area, the effort, and the catch. No public file of those reports or of the registrations was found. The purchase portal named on the licenses page was not opened. The absence of a public lookup is the finding. Sampled, supported, and held rows for both the guide registration and the vessel registration are 0. Both roster counts are null.

The licenses page says its content was last updated in May 2026. It points renewals to the online licensing system and names office phones and emails. Those contact lines were not copied.

## Association list

The Connecticut Charter and Party Boat Association boats page is a member directory, not the department registration file. The page prints the same list twice. Counted once, it has 26 headings. Seven of those headings state Waterford or Niantic. One heading states no port. New London, Groton, Noank, Mystic, and the central and western ports stay outside that seven. Mystic is a later water and was not sampled.

The seven are business or vessel headings. One of them is a fleet name rather than one hull. Passenger counts are missing on three of the seven. The two copies of one Waterford card do not print the same telephone number. Phones were not copied. The footer asserts copyright and does not grant a directory reuse. The state disclaimer does not grant commercial reuse of a license table. It limits reproduction of the state seal and the CT.gov logo. Reuse of the registration file, if one were released, is unknown.

Sampled rows equal supported rows plus held rows. Three supported rows are different named hulls at one Waterford landing. The operator's booking pages state a foot length for each. Passenger counts for the largest of the three do not agree across products, so none of those counts was treated as a certificate capacity. The association card names the fleet and does not name those three hulls. An image label on that card uses a hull name the current booking pages do not use. Those records were not joined.

The fourth supported row is a six-passenger boat. The operator page names the builder and the model and does not state a foot length. The association heading uses the same vessel name, the same six-passenger count, and Waterford. That is the one independent name match.

The held row is a Niantic party boat. The association heading is not the same name string as the operator pages, and no length was stated. Direct fetches of the operator home, about, and contact pages returned titles only. A search extract of the about page describes engines and an annual Coast Guard inspection and does not give a length. The records were not joined. None of the five was matched to a department registration number or a certificate of inspection. The Waterford landing also sells non-fishing cruises, so fishing-only was not established for that fleet. Street addresses and contact lines stay in the private packet.

## Unresolved

The highest-priority permission question is whether the department will release vessel name, landing port, and current party or charter registration status without owner addresses and telephone numbers, and whether the catch reports in section 26-157b may be redistributed. The most useful next primary source is a department records or open-data page that speaks to that registration file. The licenses page and the state disclaimer do not.

Also unresolved: whether subsection 26-31c(c) can limit a registered party boat to two customers; whether the unread purchase portal exposes any public registration status; and whether the held Niantic name can be matched to a length without treating a shared captain as one hull.
