## Two different credentials

RSA 215 is a person license. No person may guide until registered with the executive director and issued a license. A licensed guide must be at least 18 and skilled with the boats or canoes used for fishing and hunting. The license expires with the calendar year. The application section in force on this check date took effect November 1, 2026. It routes the application to the chief of the law enforcement division and requires a criminal-history check for a first-time applicant. That text was read from the official statute site.

RSA 214:9, XVI is the coastal for-hire vessel license. It is issued for each charter boat and each party boat. The owner or operator may take, possess, or transport finfish from coastal and estuarine waters. A charter boat is under 100 gross tons and meets Coast Guard requirements to carry 6 or fewer passengers for hire. A party boat or head boat holds a Coast Guard Certificate of Inspection to carry passengers for hire. A person fishing on a vessel licensed under that paragraph is exempt from the individual recreational saltwater license. The same exemption is stated on the department saltwater FAQ.

RSA 215:5, III says a person who holds the for-hire vessel license must have a Coast Guard master license or an operator of uninspected passenger vessels license, and current first aid and CPR from a course with a practical test, or a crew member on board who has that certification. While operating that vessel in coastal and estuarine waters, that person is exempt from the rest of the guide chapter. A Hampton or Rye charter can therefore be absent from the person guide list and still be a licensed for-hire vessel.

The fee rule was read in Fis 1110.09 on the official rules site. The current text, carried forward by the amendment effective February 23, 2024, sets $50 for each charter boat or other for-hire vessel with 6 or fewer passengers, except party boats, and $100 for each party boat, plus the agent fee in RSA 214-A:4. The print application, revision 05/22, shows $51 and $101 and says the license is issued to an individual. Each year the applicant must file the form and a copy of the boat registration. The form also asks for vessel name, state registration or Coast Guard number, principal port, length, and licensed capacity. Those registration numbers are not on the public list.
