# Maine fishing directory sources and reuse

Checked 2026-11-25. Scope is Casco Bay and Boothbay Harbor. This note records how the state credentials guides and coastal for-hire fishing boats, what lists are actually public, and what those pages say about commercial reuse. It does not publish a directory. Individual operators, captains, and hulls from the private sample are omitted here.

## Method

Work started at the Department of Inland Fisheries and Wildlife guide-resources page and followed the licensing program, the Secretary of State copy of the guide rule, and the statutes that govern the guide license, department license records, and the coastal for-hire license. The live Department of Marine Resources for-hire page, the Maine Office of Tourism listing rules, the Maine.gov disclaimer and link policy, and the Freedom of Access Act copy rules were opened the same day.

Two landings were opened: a downtown Boothbay Harbor marina that publishes transient slip rates for May 16 to October 11, 2026, and a South Portland marina on Portland Harbor whose homepage was accepting 2027 dockage. Neither marina page lists fishing charter boats.

A separate sample, defined below, looked for bookable fishing boats on operator sites. A current webpage, a commercial-reuse grant, and authority to claim a listing were treated as three different things. Silence on a right is recorded as unknown.

## Guide categories and the coastal charter license

Chapter 24 of the Inland Fisheries and Wildlife rules (09-137 CMR ch. 24, Word file posted July 2025, substantive amendments through filing 2021-072 on April 13, 2021) is the classification text. A guide is a person paid to accompany or assist someone in the fields, forests, or on the waters or ice while hunting, fishing, trapping, boating, snowmobiling, using an ATV, or camping at a primitive camping area. The license classifications are:

- Specialized hunting, including related camping.
- Specialized fishing, limited to inland waters, including related camping.
- Specialized recreational guiding for watercraft, ATVs, snowmobiles, and camping. Paddlesports under this classification are on inland waters.
- Specialized sea-kayaking on territorial seas and tributaries out to the three-mile limit.
- Specialized tide-water fishing for sport fishing on territorial waters out to the three-mile limit. The rule excepts species harvested under a Department of Marine Resources license. The rule does not apply to activity aboard a vessel that holds a current U.S. Coast Guard certificate of inspection.
- Master certification, an experience overlay on a classification already held. It requires a license in that classification in each of the ten years before the affidavit, and five years of professional experience inside that decade at the hour thresholds in the rule.

The same rule says a person who operates a motorboat on the state's territorial seas and tributaries to guide hunting or fishing must hold a U.S. Coast Guard license to carry passengers for hire.

The guide license itself is 12 M.R.S. §12853. It is issued to an individual, requires an examination, first aid for first-time applicants, and a state and FBI background check whose results are confidential. The fee is $135. The license expires on December 31 of the fourth complete year after issuance. The program page says there are currently approximately 6,300 licensed guides. That sentence is not dated, not split by classification, and not a roster.

Coastal paid recreational fishing is a different credential. 12 M.R.S. §6313, effective January 1, 2026 (PL 2025, c. 155), requires an individual for-hire charter boat operator's license to take paying passengers recreationally fishing in coastal waters. The license names the vessel. It does not authorize sale of the fish. There is no license fee. An agent fee of $1 or $2 may be charged. As amended by PL 2025, c. 570, the person qualifies with a U.S. Coast Guard merchant mariner credential endorsed to carry passengers for hire, or with a current tidewater fishing guide license. The compiled statute extracted October 5, 2026 already shows that amendment. Chapter 570 uses the second regular session's general effective date of July 29, 2026. The earlier eligibility wording, a merchant marine document for an uninspected-passenger-vessel operator or a master or mate license, is repealed on that compiled page.

The practical split is this. Inland fishing guidance is a Registered Maine Guide fishing classification. Tidewater sport fishing guidance is the tidewater classification, and it still sits under the Coast Guard passenger-for-hire note when a motorboat is used. A Coast Guard inspected vessel is outside the tidewater guide rule, and its paid recreational fishing still needs the Department of Marine Resources for-hire license. The for-hire license is the record that identifies a hull. The guide license identifies a person and a classification.

## What is public

No public guide roster, search tool, download, or license field dictionary was found. The guide-resources page is an exam reading list. The program page is a classification summary plus the approximate statewide count. Professional-license download tools on other state systems were not the path MDIFW uses for this license.

The Department of Marine Resources for-hire page no longer carries a fleet table. The live page says that, starting in late spring 2023, those listings moved to a Maine Office of Tourism site. Operators may apply for a Visit Maine listing. That listing is voluntary, reviewed by the tourism office, and meant to be checked by the business at least once a year. It is not a license lookup. A search index still described an older fleet table. That table was not on the page opened on the check date and was not used.

Visit Maine listings that were opened for the sample are withheld from this public file because they identify the businesses. Their terms are the tourism contributor page, not a credential.

## Sample and counts

License rosters contributed no rows. The approximate figure of 6,300 guides is a population statement, so `roster_count` stays null. The for-hire population is unknown, so that denominator stays null.

The boat sample is every fishing hull distinguished by a proper name or by a model on three operator sites offering paid fishing in Casco Bay or the Boothbay Harbor region. Several pages on one site count as one publisher.

The tourism page confirmed the business and the fishing offer and did not print that proper name. One row is held: the operator describes a skiff only by length and model, with no proper name or hull identifier, and the tourism listing does not mention it. A described model is not a unique hull.

One Casco rate block is still labeled Summer 2024. One Boothbay operation's dock does not reconcile between the operator site and the tourism listing, and that listing also mentions Casco Bay. The operator's own Boothbay pages were used for the water. None of the five rows was matched to a current guide classification or for-hire license. Those rosters are not public, so the match was not inferred.

## Commercial reuse

Maine.gov allows links to state pages. The linker may not frame those pages or present them as the linker's own content, and may not imply state endorsement. The content disclaimer allocates risk for reliance on the site and routes copyright notices to the portal vendor. Neither page grants a commercial directory, a bulk download, or an attribution rule for license records.

12 M.R.S. §10111, effective with the July 29, 2026 general date for that session's nonemergency laws, makes personally identifiable information in Inland Fisheries and Wildlife license and application records confidential. That includes telephone number, email, physical address, the department's online licensing ID, date of birth, driver's license number, Social Security number, and taxpayer identification number. The department may disseminate data that is not personally identifiable, and that dissemination may include names and mailing addresses from a license. It may charge reasonable fees for individual records or for a whole or partial database, and those fees are not capped by the Freedom of Access Act fee rules. The statute does not require the department to publish a roster. It does not say that a recipient may republish the extract in a commercial directory. Attribution and redistribution limits are unknown.

The Freedom of Access Act, 1 M.R.S. §408-A, still governs inspection and copying of records that are public. A request need not be in writing. The agency acknowledges it within five working days. The commercial-interest language in that section is a fee-waiver test. It is not a license to republish confidential license files. Section 10111 is the more specific rule for this department's license databases.

The for-hire statute creates a vessel-specific license and does not publish it. Whether the vessel field can be released is unknown. Landings statistics are a different, confidential dataset and were not used as a boat list.

Tourism contributors submit their own Visit Maine listings and must have rights to the media they upload. That is permission to appear on Visit Maine. It is not permission for another directory to copy the listing. Operator sites that were opened had no terms page granting commercial reuse. Marina pages were likewise silent, aside from a reserved-rights footer on the South Portland marina. Silence is unknown, not a grant.

A listing on a tourism page, a statutory right to ask for a public record, and authority to claim a Rate Your Guide listing are separate. None of the pages checked supplies the last of those.

## Unresolved questions

The question that has to be answered before a Maine directory can be built is whether Inland Fisheries and Wildlife will disseminate guide names and classifications, without confidential personally identifiable information, for commercial directory use under §10111, and whether that dissemination limits redistribution. The paired question is whether Marine Resources will release the vessel named on each current for-hire charter operator license.

Also unresolved: the number of tidewater guides, the number of for-hire licenses, any license-to-hull match for the private sample, the conflicting Boothbay dock, and the rate block that is still labeled Summer 2024.

## First source

Ask Inland Fisheries and Wildlife, under 12 M.R.S. §10111, for a classification-filtered guide extract. Ask Marine Resources for the vessel identified on each current for-hire charter operator license. The guide extract names people and classifications. The for-hire record is the one that names a coastal fishing boat. Visit Maine remains a voluntary crosscheck after those records exist, not a substitute for them.
