# Michigan fishing directory inventory

Checked 2026-11-25. Water in scope: Traverse City charter trips. This note records the licensing lookups, the reuse terms that were actually printed, and aggregate sample counts. Individual boats, captains, and booking sites are not named here.

No one was contacted. No listing was published.

## What was found

Michigan publishes a person credential for inland guiding and a separate inspection path for boats that carry passengers for hire. Neither one is a Traverse City departure list.

The inland sport fishing guide license is a person license. The program page says that as of March 1, 2024 a person who acts as a sport fishing guide on inland lakes and streams, including Michigan boundary waters, needs this license and a valid fishing license. The fee is $150 for a resident and $300 for a nonresident. The term is three years from issuance. A U.S. Coast Guard captain's license waives the fee. If the person operates a watercraft, the page also requires a state inland pilot license or a Coast Guard captain's license. Monthly catch reports start April 1, 2024, including months with no trips. Reports exclude the Great Lakes, the connecting waters named on the page, and waters under five acres. A Pere Marquette River commercial fish guide permit is a separate capped permit. The page says no new permits of that kind are being issued. That river is not Traverse City.

The posted name list is a 21-page PDF headed Updated November 23, 2026. It says 187 applications were submitted in 2025 and 120 licenses were issued under this section in 2025. Columns are last name, first name, and year licensed. An automated line parse, skipping header lines that also contain a year, counted 746 name rows: 525 labeled 2024, 120 labeled 2025, and 101 labeled 2026. The 2025 count matches the printed issuance figure. The parsed name strings did not repeat. This is a line parse, not a hand audit. There is no city, port, or boat. The 746 rows are people who hold a current three-year license. They are not the 187 applications, and they are not boats leaving Traverse City. Names were not copied.

Compiled MCL 324.48714a, displayed as complete through Public Act 114 of 2026, does not use the PDF's citation. Subsection (8) says catch-report information is confidential and exempt from the Freedom of Information Act. Subsection (9) is the duty to post each year the previous year's application count, the previous year's issuance count, and a list of individuals with valid licenses. The PDF says it fulfills subsection (8). Both texts were read on the same day.

Great Lakes charter fishing is a different program. The operators page says charter operators are identified from the previous year's catch reports and from certificates of inspection, and that new operators must identify themselves. It defines a charter excursion as one completed boat trip. It says summaries for selected ports, lake totals, and annual reports are prepared each year. The file it links as the 2025 charter report is one page of lake totals. Printed excursions are Lake Michigan 12,481, Lake Huron including the St. Marys River 2,937, Lake Superior 924, Lake Erie including the Detroit River 2,619, and Lake St. Clair including the St. Clair River 1,782. Lake Michigan angler hours are 264,080 and Lake Michigan anglers are 51,311. The word Traverse does not appear. Those excursions are not a count of licensed hulls and not a Traverse City count. A sport-trolling license is required on that page only for lake trout charter fishing. The statute section for that license was not opened.

The public Great Lakes charter page is a voluntary business directory. It says the list is updated periodically, that visitors should verify it, and that inclusion is not a recommendation or endorsement. The table has 397 data rows across 92 city labels. Traverse City is the city label on 18 rows and 18 business names. Each of those 18 uses the lake label Michigan. The table has a captain column and contact columns. It has no length, builder, or hull name. Contact fields were not copied. A city label is not proof of where a boat left the dock.

Vessels carrying passengers for hire are inspected by class. On navigable waters, including the Great Lakes, the page requires a Coast Guard captain's license. Inland waters require a state pilot license. The certificate of inspection is downloaded from the vessel owner's account. Starting in 2026 the page says the certificate is electronic. No public inspection roster was found. No account was created.

Duncan L. Clinch Marina, 111 E. Grandview Parkway, is a city marina under the Michigan State Waterways Commission. A direct download returned HTTP 403. A second fetch returned the page. It lists slips and a state reservation system. It does not list charter boats.

## Traverse City sample

Three operator booking pages were opened beyond the statewide agency pages. One boat section describes one hull. A second page names one hull for Traverse City trips and also prints another town's office address. A third operator's about page names four boats. One of those four was sampled. All three sampled hulls stay held.

The holds are the same kind. No opened page showed a documentation or inspection number. A tourism listing repeated one operator's first-person wording, so it was not a second measurement. A booking marketplace returned HTTP 403 to a direct request. Wording from a search extract of that marketplace was not treated as an independent measurement, including where it used different sentences for a length and a builder. One operator's own pages disagree on which side of Grand Traverse Bay the sampled boat uses, and they do not both name the builder. A second, shorter boat appears only in the marketplace extract and was not given a row. A state tourism property page returned a bot interstitial and then HTTP 403 and was not used.

The three holds are not added to the 746 names, the 397 directory rows, or the 18 Traverse City directory rows. The licensed Traverse City departure population is null.

## Reuse

DNR website policy says DNR websites and applications are for personal and non-commercial use only. It says content may not be copied, published, sold, or transferred unless the law provides otherwise or the State gives prior written permission. A linking exception is stated. Content is provided as is.

Michigan.gov policy says data derived from the site may not be used commercially or resold unless a separate written agreement allows it, or department-specific terms provided with the data allow it. Disclaimer blocks on the retrieved page are marked Updated: December 2021. The page also forbids copying that implies official approval. The DNR policy is the department-specific text found for these pages, and it does not allow a commercial directory.

The duty in MCL 324.48714a(9) to post counts and names is not that permission. Subsection (8) withholds the catch reports. The FOIA process page was opened. The extract did not yield a readable request paragraph. No request was sent. Whether an inspection roster would be released is unknown.

One operator page prints a 2026 copyright claim and no reuse license. Other operator pages were silent in the text that was retrieved. Those pages are not covered by the DNR policy.

## Unresolved

The citation mismatch is unresolved. The PDF cites subsection (8). The compiled section assigns the public list to subsection (9) and confidentiality to subsection (8).

Also unresolved: whether any public file lists current certificates of inspection. The certificate path that was read is the owner's login. The FOIA page did not answer the release question.

The charter page's port summaries were not in the 2025 PDF. The most useful next primary source is the 2024 charter summary and the fisheries report archive. The association page linked from the charter operators page was not opened. Compiled MCL 324.47328, the sport-trolling section named by the program's license sentence, was not opened.
