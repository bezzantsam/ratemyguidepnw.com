# South Dakota fishing-directory sources and reuse

Checked 2026-11-25. Scope is Lake Oahe near Pierre: guide credentials, the public license path, boat-title access, and one state access site on the reservoir. This note records what each publisher states about reuse and the aggregate result of one defined sample. It does not name people, businesses, license numbers, or client records. Private evidence stays outside this repository.

No one was contacted. Nothing here is a listing, a claim, or a publishing decision.

## Four records, kept apart

A paid fishing trip on Lake Oahe can touch four different records. A booking page does not establish any of them.

**Angler fishing license.** The department's general license-requirements page is an angler, turtle, bullfrog, and bait license page. A fishing license is required to take fish. It is not transferable. The Go Outdoors address linked for purchases is a customer login. It asks for a date of birth, a last name, and a personal identifier such as the last four digits of a Social Security number. No identifier was submitted. No customer row was opened.

**Hunting guide.** Codified Law 41-4-12, read from the legislature's statute service, defines a hunting guide as a person who holds himself or herself out to the public as a hunting guide and, for compensation, directs or provides services for hunting any wild animal. Section 41-4-13 bars that person from guiding a hunting activity on state game production areas, state parks, recreation areas, lakeside use areas, specified federal land leased to the state and posted as a game production area, private land leased to the department for public access, and certain highway rights-of-way. A right-of-way next to land the guide owns or leases is the stated exception. A violation is a Class 1 misdemeanor. The source line is 1998. The department FAQ states that Game, Fish and Parks does not license hunting or fishing guides, and it restates a hunting-guide land ban. The FAQ's land list is not identical to the statute sentence. The statute is the prohibition that was read.

**Fishing guide.** No codified fishing-guide license was found in the sections read. The FAQ states that the department does not license fishing guides. House Bill 1215 in 2025, and House Bill 1122 and Senate Bill 160 in 2026, were proposals to create licensure or registration. Each was deferred to the 41st legislative day. Those bills are not current law. A booking page that says "guide" is a role label. It is not a state fishing-guide credential, and it is not the hunting-guide definition in section 41-4-12.

**The water, the landing, and the hull.** Spring Creek Recreation Area is a department site on Lake Oahe, 15 miles northwest of Pierre, with a boat ramp, a marina, and a concessionaire for the store and restaurant. An entrance license is required. Hunting is prohibited there year-round. The lakeside-use index lists many areas statewide and does not label which rows are Lake Oahe, so no reservoir-wide ramp total was taken. A listed ramp is an operator's departure only when that operator's page names it. Boat registration is a county-treasurer transaction under the Department of Revenue. The department may furnish a registration abstract for a two-dollar written request and a title-history abstract for a five-dollar written request. The registration-abstract source line begins in 1959. The latest cited amendment is 2021. Neither request was sent. No public hull search was found. A described boat, including an insurance sentence or a brand in an image label, is not a named hull.

## What was available, and what was not

The saved lead, the general fishing-license requirements page, does not list guides. The FAQ answer quoted above is the department's published position. The customer login is not a roster. The roster count for fishing guides is null because the department states that it does not issue that license.

Boat numbering is described on the department's boat-licensing page. Motorboats of any length, including electric trolling motors, and boats over 12 feet, must be registered to operate on public waters. Registration is annual, at the county treasurer's office in the county of residence. A vessel documented with the Coast Guard and on South Dakota waters for fewer than 60 consecutive days in a calendar year is described as outside South Dakota registration. A vessel under a South Dakota marina contract for 60 or more consecutive days is described as needing South Dakota registration. The revenue department's boat page is an owner application and renewal path. It does not offer a public search by hull or by name. No abstract was requested.

One additional operator host failed before it rendered. It was not retried and is not a row. A Corps of Engineers Lake Oahe project address returned HTTP 403 and was not read, so no Corps commercial-use term was retrieved.

## Reuse

The department disclaimer says anyone may view, copy, or distribute information on the state website for personal or informational use without owing an obligation to the state. The same page says the state does not warrant that the materials are free of copyright claims or other limits on free use. That sentence permits personal or informational copying. It does not grant commercial-directory reuse, it does not state an attribution line, and it does not describe a bulk feed or a permission desk. The page footer asserts copyright 2026 in the State of South Dakota. The disclaimer says a later statewide text is posted at sd.gov. That statewide page returned only a site title on this check and was not read.

The department privacy policy says no personal information is collected unless a person provides it for an online activity. It points to a statewide privacy page as the later text. That statewide page also returned only a site title and was not read. The policy that was read does not grant reuse of license status. No license status was retrieved.

Codified Law 32-3A-14 and 32-3A-38 allow a written abstract of boat registration or title history for a stated fee. An abstract is an access path. It was not requested. The sections do not grant commercial-directory reuse. Codified Law 1-27-1 was located and not read. Its catchline is public records open to inspection and copying. The section text is the next permission source.

One operator page that rendered asserts all rights reserved for 2026. Another rendered operator page is silent on reuse. Silence is unknown. No marketing copy is repeated here.

No source checked on this pass grants bulk commercial reuse of a named guide list. No list exists in the sources that were read, and none was downloaded.

## Unresolved

The permission question is whether personal or informational copying under the state disclaimer, and inspection under the open-records chapter, authorize commercial-directory reuse of access-site facts or of any future guide record. The most useful next primary source is Codified Law 1-27-1 at the legislature statute service. The catchline was retrieved. The section text was not read.

Also unresolved: whether any later codified section creates a fishing-guide license that the FAQ does not reflect. The full code was not searched as a single query. Which Lake Oahe ramp, if any, is the departure for the held bookings. Whether the revenue department will furnish a hull identifier in a registration abstract without a home address. Corps commercial-use terms for the reservoir, after the project page returned HTTP 403. The statewide privacy and disclaimer pages that the department links as the later texts.
