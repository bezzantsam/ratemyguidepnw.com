# Vermont fishing-source inventory

Checked 2026-11-25. This note records the land-use commercial license, the absence of a fishing-guide registration, and a five-candidate sample aimed at Lake Champlain departures in Vermont. Candidate rows stay out of the repository. Batten Kill was not sampled.

## Credentials that are not a charter roster

The saved lead is Forests, Parks and Recreation guidance on commercial use of state recreation lands. The page was read on this check. An earlier project note that the direct read was unavailable is stale. A recreation business on state-owned land needs a commercial use license or a special use permit when the activity is organized or publicized, involves a fee or a requested donation, or would significantly affect a site or natural resources. Special use permits are short-term and non-recurring. Commercial licenses cover for-profit and non-profit use for an extended or recurring period. Proof of liability insurance is required. Fees start at $50 per permit. The same sentence says guided fishing, hunting, and trapping involving groups of less than ten people on Agency of Natural Resources lands does not require that license or permit. The leases page repeats the exemption and defines commercial use as charging a fee, renting equipment, selling a good or service, or paid advertising that generates business. It names outfitter and guide businesses as examples. Neither page lists holders.

The Fish and Wildlife lands rule is a different instrument, and it does not apply to fishing access areas. On the lands it covers, guiding for fishing, hunting, and trapping by a person is an authorized activity when it stays inside hunting, fishing, trapping, wildlife viewing, and related boating. Other commercial activity is prohibited unless the Commissioner grants a special use permit, lease, or license. The rule defines a group as ten or more people. The department's lands page says commercial activity may occur without a permit when the group has less than ten participants, and with a special use permit when the group is larger than ten. Exactly ten people is not placed the same way in those two texts. The page is not a permit holder list.

Fishing access areas are regulated by Appendix section 115. Commercial activity is prohibited except for people and entities taking part in angling, ice fishing, launching a vessel used for fishing, launching a motorboat, trapping, hunting, and the parking that goes with those uses. Group use of ten or more people, or two or more vehicles, needs authorization. The access-area page says an activity that the rules do not address can be requested by special permit at least 30 days ahead. The rule does not create a guide roster.

No fishing-guide registration was found in the license chapter that was read. A fishing license entitles the holder to take fish. Persons 15 and older need one, with the stated exceptions. The license center does not offer a guide product. A fee-fishing permit is for a private pond. A fishing-tournament permit is for a contest. A moose guide permit is a hunting document. The commercial angling rule is a fish-seller endorsement plus a fishing license, and a fish-buyer permit for buying harvested fish for resale. Buyer reports break out the share from Lake Champlain. That rule is not a for-hire boat list. None of those files was found as a public download.

A catalog search on the state open-data portal returned no dataset for guide, outfitter, charter, or commercial use. The only fishing hit is a 2018 Fish and Wildlife file whose columns are traffic-stop fields. Its rows were not downloaded. It is not a license roster.

Lake Champlain reciprocity is an angler rule. A New York fishing license may be used in the Vermont portion of the lake when New York extends the same right. Appendix section 114a and section 4609 draw that portion. The line does not list guides. A departure on the New York shore was not treated as a Vermont observation. A private marina is not the same place as an Agency of Natural Resources parcel, so the commercial-use license and the under-ten exemption do not automatically attach to a Shelburne departure.

## Terms

Vermont.gov asserts copyright for 2026. Its photo policy allows a browser print for personal, non-commercial use and refuses commercial use of the photographs. The disclaimer does not grant reuse of a license table. The statute pages say the text online is an unofficial convenience copy. The legislature disclaimer, read as a search extract, disclaims warranties and does not grant a directory. Silence on bulk reuse of a commercial-license file means unknown. Operator pages are aggregated below without their addresses. One of those sites asserts copyright and forbids copying. The others that were opened do not state a directory license.

Sampled rows equal supported rows plus held rows. The supported row is one large boat with a Shelburne home port. The operator's vessel page and rate card state the same length and the same beam, a six-person maximum, and private trips. A reports page on the same site repeats that length. Season sentences on the site do not agree and were not used. No registration number or certificate was opened. Two captains are named, and neither is assigned to this hull.

The first hold is the second large boat at the same port. The rate card states one length and one beam. A reports page states a different length for a second boat of the same brand and does not use the rate card's label. Those measurements were not joined, and the reports-page length was not opened as an extra hull.

The second hold is an open boat on the same rate card, with its own prices. A 2026 post on the operator site says that boat continues after the large boats stop. The location page says it launches from several places. No single Vermont landing was stated.

The third hold is a separate operator. The about page states a length, a builder, and a capacity that includes the captain. It does not name a landing. A reseller's ramp sentence was not adopted.

The fourth hold is another operator that offers Lake Champlain trips and states a Coast Guard license and a New York guide license. The pages that were opened do not state a hull length or a Vermont landing. The New York license was not checked. Other waters are also offered, so the trips are not established as fishing-only on Champlain. A directory link for a Charlotte operator did not return a charter page, and a reseller length for that name was not used.

## Unresolved

The highest-priority permission question is whether Forests, Parks and Recreation or Fish and Wildlife will release the name, activity, and location of a current commercial-use or special-use authorization without personal contact fields, and whether guiding for fewer than ten people is recorded at all. The most useful next primary source is a records or open-data page for that authorization file. The leases page and the open-data catalog do not publish it.

Also unresolved: whether the two lengths for the second large boat are one hull; whether the open boat's launches are in Vermont; whether the cabin boat has a Vermont landing; and whether a group of exactly ten people needs a permit on Fish and Wildlife lands.
