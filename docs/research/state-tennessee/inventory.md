# Tennessee inventory: guide licenses, Norris Lake, and the Clinch

Checked 2026-11-25. This note is a source inventory for a Tennessee fishing directory. It records the guide credential, the absence of a public roster, commercial-reuse terms, and aggregate sample counts. Candidate rows stay in the private packet. Two operator domains are personal names and are omitted from this file.

## Method

The starting point was the TWRA license and permit page. The miscellaneous-license application, the Secretary of State compilation of the license-fee rules, the TN.gov linking policy, the DMCA notice page, the TWRA contact page, the TWRA public-records rule, and the Comptroller public-records FAQ were opened from that lead. The GoOutdoorsTennessee homepage was opened because the license page sends guide purchases there.

Norris Lake and the Clinch were kept apart. The TWRA trout-regulation page, the Norris Dam State Park boating page, and a Norris marina location page were opened. Five bookable fishing observations were read from operator pages: one Norris boat with a stated length and builder, two lengths on a second Norris page, one Norris booking page that does not identify a boat, and one Clinch tailwater drift boat. A Clinch page that says charters are not currently offered was opened and kept out of the sample. No one was contacted. Tourism roundups were not used as rosters.

A row is supported only when two publishers agree on the same physical hull. A boat description, a captain, or a brand, without a second publisher and without a registration or documentation number, stays held. Sampled rows equal supported plus held on every coverage row.

## License, holder, and hull

The license page and Tenn. Comp. R. & Regs. 1660-01-28-.10 describe one guide product:

- The guide license is held by the individual who accepts compensation for assisting another person in hunting, fishing, or trapping.
- The resident fee is $150. The nonresident fee is $650.
- An employer may buy those person licenses for its employees.
- The application, Type 450 and Type 455, asks the holder to declare hunt, fish, or both. It asks for a business name. It does not ask for a boat.

Authority for the rule is T.C.A. § 70-1-206. The original rule was filed March 27, 2015, and effective June 25, 2015. Amendments were filed March 21, 2023, and effective June 19, 2023. The compilation that was read is the July 2026 revision. Its PDF was created July 22, 2026. A direct request for the canonical rules path returned HTTP 403. The opened file came from the alternate rules path. A deer-bait section in that same file is effective July 22, 2026. That date is not a 2026 change to the guide rule.

The application file was created April 11, 2023, and modified December 3, 2025. It says a Social Security number is required to buy a Tennessee hunting or fishing license. The number was not copied. Nothing on the form, the license page, or the rule says the guide license covers the customer's fishing license. One Norris operator page says the angler still needs a Tennessee fishing license.

Two other products on the license page are not this credential. Commercial fishing licenses are harvest licenses: resident $244, nonresident $1,220, resident senior at no charge, and a helper license at $244. The Annual NCWMA off-highway-vehicle guide permit is $700 and is charged in addition to any other license required for that guided activity.

## What is not a roster

No public search, download, or field dictionary of current guide licenses was found. The statewide current denominator is unknown.

GoOutdoorsTennessee is the purchase portal named on the license page. The homepage offers licensing, boat registration, events, quota hunts, and harvest reporting. Account links point at a customer-lookup URL. That lookup was not opened. The homepage does not list guide holders. Boat registration is a separate module from the guide license.

TN.gov linking policy allows a copy of TN.gov information when the copy is not misleading, does not imply state endorsement, and identifies the agency and the agency web address. Linking does not require permission. Copying a TN.gov graphic onto another site does. The policy is not a license to republish a holder file. The DMCA page is a takedown notice process.

The TWRA contact page will provide existing records that are subject to the Open Records Act and says the agency is not required to create a summary or a spreadsheet. Chapter 1660-03-02, dated June 2019, limits inspection to a citizen of Tennessee, allows proof of citizenship, charges for copies and labor, waives those fees at or under $10, requires an advance when estimated reproduction costs exceed $10, and requires redaction of confidential information. The Comptroller FAQ states the same citizen limit under T.C.A. § 10-7-503(a)(2)(A) and says a photo identification may be required. A request was not filed. A copy under that rule would still not, by itself, authorize a commercial listing.

## Norris Lake and the Clinch

TWRA trout regulations treat the Clinch from Norris Dam downstream to the Highway 61 bridge, including tributaries, as its own trout section: a 14–20 inch protected length range, a seven-trout creel, and only one trout longer than 20 inches. That tailwater is not the reservoir above the dam.

Norris Dam State Park's boating page says Norris Dam Marina, near the dam, has a boat ramp and pontoon rentals. Guide services are mentioned only as something to ask the marina about by telephone. No guide and no hull is named. The park footer reserves all rights. The privacy page describes collection of personal information and does not grant directory reuse.

A Twin Cove marina location page names one guide business and prints that business's domain. The page's structured data was published December 13, 2013, and modified April 28, 2021. The sentence was still on the page on this check date. The marina page does not describe a hull. No terms page was found.

## Open question

The blocking permission question is whether TWRA will release the current guide-license file, limited to fish or both declarations and without Social Security numbers or street addresses, and whether that file contains a vessel identifier. The most useful next primary source is the public-records process in chapter 1660-03-02. The private packet lists the commercial-fishing application and the two GoOutdoors account portals that were linked and not opened.
