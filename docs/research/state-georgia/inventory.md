# Georgia inventory: guide licenses and Golden Isles departures

Checked 2026-11-25. This note is a source inventory for a Georgia fishing directory. It records credentials, the absence of a public roster, commercial-reuse terms, and aggregate sample counts. Candidate rows stay in the private packet.

## Method

The starting point was the Department of Natural Resources 2026-2027 charter/guide packet linked from the Coastal Resources Division commercial fishing page. The statute text, the reporting rule, the Department website policy, and the open-records form were opened from that lead. A 2017 survey page was opened because it is the only guide count the license pages point toward.

For Brunswick and St. Simons departures, the Morningstar Marinas Golden Isles location page was opened as a landing. Three bookable fishing publishers that serve St. Simons Island, Jekyll Island, or Brunswick were opened. A burial-at-sea page on a related site was opened and kept out of the fishing sample. No one was contacted. Tourism roundups and booking marketplaces were not used as rosters.

A row is supported only when two publishers agree on the same physical hull. A boat name, a captain, or a brand, without a second publisher and without a registration or documentation number, stays held. Sampled rows equal supported plus held on every coverage row.

## License, holder, and hull

O.C.G.A. 27-2-23.2 and the 2026-2027 packet describe three different products:

- The saltwater fishing guide license is held by the captain. The packet prices it at $25 for a resident and $50 for a nonresident.
- The customer fishing license is optional and is also held by that captain. The statute allows it when the guide has a U.S. Coast Guard operator-of-passenger-vessel license. It covers fee-paying anglers while they are fishing with that guide. Resident prices on the packet are $150 for up to six passengers and $400 for an unlimited number. The nonresident customer license on the packet is $400. This is not a hull license.
- A saltwater fishing pier license is a hotel or motel pier product. It is not a charter boat.

The packet adds a $5 transaction fee for each license. The license year printed for the 2026 packet runs April 1, 2026 through March 31, 2027. Purchase paths printed on the packet are mail, email, or in person at Coastal Regional Headquarters in Brunswick.

The blank form asks for captain identity and for up to two vessels: trailered or not, Georgia registration or Coast Guard documentation number, owner, name or make, and length. Those fields are collected. They are not published. A trip page that says a fishing license is included does not say whether the product is the guide license or the customer license.

Rule 391-2-4-.16, as indexed on the Secretary of State rules site on this date, requires licensed guides to submit activity records. The indexed text sets a monthly due date when the Department requires the report. Those reports were not downloaded. A direct download of the rule page returned HTTP 403. The rules-site footer reserves Fastcase rights in the compilation.

## What is not a roster

No public search, download, or field dictionary of current guide licenses was found. The statewide current denominator is unknown.

The Coastal Resources page for the 2017 saltwater angler and guide survey says all 134 state-licensed resident saltwater fishing guides were invited. That count belongs to 2017. It is not the 2026 fleet.

The Department website policy does not grant commercial directory reuse. It points disclosure of personal information to the Georgia Open Records Act. The open-records form at the Department allows inspection and copying of non-exempt records and allows an hourly charge. A request was not filed. A copy under that Act would still not, by itself, authorize a commercial listing.

Morningstar Marinas Golden Isles publishes a St. Simons landing page and does not name a charter boat on it. The marina terms say site material may not be copied, distributed, republished, uploaded, posted, or transmitted without prior written consent.

## Open question

The blocking permission question is whether the Department will release the current guide and customer-license file, with the customer-license tier and the vessel identifier, for commercial directory use. The most useful next primary source is the Department open-records form, limited to license type and hull identifiers. The private packet lists first-party pages that were linked and not opened, including the Saltwater Information Program page, the federal permit index linked from the commercial fishing page, and the prior-year guide packet.
