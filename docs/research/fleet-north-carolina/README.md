# North Carolina for-hire sources and commercial reuse

Checked 2026-11-25. This note records where a North Carolina fishing directory could look for Outer Banks charter boats, who holds each credential, and what each publisher actually allows. It is a source review. It does not publish listings.

Candidate rows, hull notes, crew names, and operator-site URLs for the defined boat sample stay in the private research packet. This file reports methodology, scoped counts, publisher links, permission terms, and open questions.

## Coastal licenses

The licensing page is the [N.C. Division of Marine Fisheries for-hire licenses](https://www.deq.nc.gov/about/divisions/marine-fisheries/licenses-permits-and-leases/hire-fishing-licenses) page. The statute is [G.S. 113-174.3](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_113/GS_113-174.3.html). Three products authorize a coastal for-hire operation:

1. Blanket For-Hire Captain's Coastal Recreational Fishing License. Held by a person who has a U.S. Coast Guard license suitable for the passenger rating. It covers passengers' coastal recreational fishing while that captain is in command. Each boat operated under it also needs a for-hire vessel license or a Commercial Fishing Vessel Registration with a for-hire endorsement.
2. Blanket For-Hire Vessel Coastal Recreational Fishing License. Held by the vessel. The applicant may be the operator, owner, or a business agent. A Coast Guard license is required to operate and is not required to purchase the license. It covers passengers in coastal waters. A vessel with this license does not also need the for-hire endorsement on a Commercial Fishing Vessel Registration.
3. Non-Blanket For-Hire Vessel License. Held by the vessel. It does not cover passengers. Each angler who must hold a coastal recreational fishing license still needs one. The public fee table shows one price for this product. The vessel application still asks whether the boat carries six or fewer passengers. This review does not treat that form question as a second published fee.

Published blanket fees on the Division page: resident $250 for six or fewer passengers and $350 for more than six; nonresident $312.50 and $437.50. Non-blanket vessel fee on the same table: resident $25 and nonresident $37.50. An individual coastal recreational fishing license does not authorize for-hire operation. A block of ten-day licenses cannot be used on a for-hire vessel.

The [vessel application revised 2025-12](https://www.deq.nc.gov/hire-vessel-coastal-recreational-fishing-license-application/open) is a blank form, not a roster. Its survey categories are charter boat, head boat, and guide boat. The captain's application packet is sent on request and was not a public download.

No public holder list, hull list, or field dictionary of current for-hire licenses was found. Aggregate sales tables in Division statistical reports were not used as a vessel count. The statewide for-hire denominator is unknown.

[G.S. 113-170.3](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_113/GS_113-170.3.html) makes licensee-kept records, and Division compilations of business operations, vessels owned or operated, capacity, gear, catch, and fishing location, confidential. They are not public records under Chapter 132. Aggregate statistics that do not identify a licensee are public. Whether a bare list of license type, person versus vessel, and hull identifier is releasable is unresolved.

A marina statement that the charter rate includes the fishing license does not identify which of the three products is held, or whether the holder is the captain or the boat.

## Federal documents and the same boat

Federal for-hire permits that can name a vessel, by vessel name and official number, are separate from the state license:

- [South Atlantic snapper-grouper charter/headboat](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/permit/south-atlantic-charter-headboat-snapper-grouper-hire-fishing-permit-open-access), open access, vessel.
- [Atlantic dolphin/wahoo charter/headboat](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/permit/atlantic-charter-headboat-dolphin-wahoo-hire-fishing-permit-open-access), open access, vessel.
- [Coastal migratory pelagics charter/headboat](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/permit/atlantic-charter-headboat-coastal-migratory-pelagics-hire-fishing-permit-open-access), open access, vessel.
- [Atlantic HMS charter/headboat](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/permit/atlantic-highly-migratory-species-charter-headboat-permit-open-access), open access, vessel. The page states a $24 fee.

The [Southeast frequent FOIA page](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/southeast/resources-fishing/frequent-freedom-information-act-requests-southeast-region), updated on that site through March 2026, points permit-holder lists to the Southeast FOIA portal. On 2026-11-25 that portal returned a short HTML shell and no downloadable for-hire roster. The [Southeast Region Headboat Survey vessel directory](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/inport/item/27646) is described as password-restricted. Its rows were not used.

The [Greater Atlantic permits page](https://apps-garfo.fisheries.noaa.gov/permits/) publishes `permits_2026.csv`, a commercial vessel-permit summary. The file checked on 2026-11-25 has 3,926 data rows. It covers Greater Atlantic commercial permits. It is a different population from South Atlantic charter/headboat permits. Of those rows, 106 list a North Carolina home port. That 106 is not a North Carolina for-hire fleet count. An exact-name check of the six coastal sample boats produced zero same-hull matches. Two names exist on different hulls in other states. Four names are absent. Owner telephone numbers and street addresses in that file were not copied.

## Inland guide credentials

[G.S. 113-270.4](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_113/GS_113-270.4.html) requires a current Wildlife Resources Commission hunting and fishing guide license before a person serves for hire as a hunting or fishing guide. The license is individual. Fees printed in the statute are $15 resident and $150 nonresident. A [proposed amendment to 15A NCAC 10A .1601](https://www.ncwildlife.gov/media/4952/open) shows different figures in strikeout and in the proposal. The fee actually charged on 2026-11-25 was not confirmed. The statute text is not limited on its face to inland waters. Coastal for-hire operations are separately required by G.S. 113-174.3. Whether a coastal captain must hold both was not resolved. No public guide roster was found.

The [Commercial Use Permit](https://www.ncwildlife.org/commercial-activity-permit) is a different credential for paid activity on game lands and boating access areas. The page states $100 per year plus $3 per participant. It is not a hull license and not the guide license.

Inland fishing licenses and coastal recreational fishing licenses cover different waters. Joint waters can be covered by either. That boundary was not used to assign a license to any sampled boat.

## Commercial reuse

North Carolina agency pages checked here use the [nc.gov disclaimer and terms of use](https://www.nc.gov/disclaimer-terms-use). The operative sentence is:

> Unless otherwise noted on an individual document, the State of North Carolina grants permission to copy and distribute non-image files, documents, and information for non-commercial use, provided they are copied and distributed without alteration and appropriately cite to the source.

The same page prohibits use of photos and other images on state websites. There is no commercial directory grant and no commercial-permission workflow. Chapter 132 is an inspection right. It is not a grant to build a commercial directory from a licensee file. The [DEQ public-records page](https://www.deq.nc.gov/accessdeq/requesting-public-records) is the published request path. A request was not filed. A future release would still need its own reuse decision, and a lookup is not authority to claim a listing.

[NOAA Fisheries website policies](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/national/about-us/website-policies-and-disclaimers) say:

> Information created by the U.S. government and presented on U.S. government websites is not subject to copyright in the United States. We encourage the use of such information for non-commercial, scientific, and educational purposes.

The page requests this acknowledgement when NOAA Fisheries material is copied: `Courtesy: National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration.` Externally produced material needs the copyright holder's written permission. Absence of U.S. copyright is not a grant to republish personal contact fields or to claim a charter listing.

[Oregon Inlet terms](https://www.oregon-inlet.com/terms-conditions/) cover reservations, deposits, cancellation, and the statement that the next year's fleet and prices are not final until the marina announces them. The site footer states a 2022 copyright. The terms do not grant directory reuse. Reuse status: unknown.

Pirate's Cove published no terms, privacy, or copyright page in the pages checked. Reuse status: unknown. [Smarter Charters](https://smartercharters.com/) is the booking widget both marinas embed. Its homepage has no terms link. The vendor is an access path. It is not the rights holder for the boat descriptions. Reuse status: unknown.

Six independent operator sites were opened for the six coastal sample boats. Two carry an all-rights-reserved footer and do not grant directory reuse. The other four are silent or did not render terms. The inland guide page has no terms link. Reuse status there: unknown. No operator was contacted.

A current booking page, a government non-commercial copying term, and a public-records statute are three different things. None of them authorizes Rate Your Guide to claim a listing.

## First source and the open question

The most useful current list of named Outer Banks hulls is the Oregon Inlet live booking dropdown: 47 names on 2026-11-25. It is a booking list. It is not a license roster. Next year's fleet is not final under the marina's own terms. Commercial reuse waits on a grant from the marina.

The permission question that blocks a statewide directory is whether the Division of Marine Fisheries will release the current for-hire license file, with credential type, person versus vessel, and hull identifier, for commercial directory use, or whether G.S. 113-170.3 withholds that file. The nc.gov terms grant only non-commercial copying of pages the state has already published.
