# South Carolina for-hire fishing sources

Checked 2026-11-25. This note records how a saltwater for-hire fishing listing would be sourced, what passenger class changes, and what reuse terms were actually published. It is not a fleet directory. Hull names, people, credential numbers, and contact details stay out of this file. Candidate evidence is kept outside the repository.

The sample is one Murrells Inlet landing, Crazy Sister Marina. Charleston uses the same statewide saltwater charter license. No Charleston landing was opened. No inland outfitter was sampled. The Title 50 chapters that were read do not create a freshwater fishing-guide license or a public guide roster.

## How a listing would be supported

A current lookup, a commercial-reuse grant, and authority to claim a listing are three separate facts. None of the government pages opened supplies all three.

The practical path is the operator's own fishing-booking page, cross-checked to a second publisher such as the landing that sells the same trip. The state license is the passenger-class credential. It is not a public roster to copy.

Statewide license count: unknown. No name list, download, or open-data field dictionary was linked from the licensing, privacy, FOIA, or reporting pages that were opened.

## Passenger class

The state license and the Coast Guard class answer different questions. Carrying fishermen for hire adds the state license. Carrying any passenger for hire raises the Coast Guard operator credential. A federal fishery permit is a third, species-specific vessel permit.

| Class | Who holds it | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SCDNR charter fishing vessel license, S.C. Code §50-9-540(D) | Owner or operator, one annual license for each vessel | 6 or fewer passengers: $275 resident / $550 nonresident. 7 to 49: $450 / $900. 50 or more: $650 / $1,300. |
| Coast Guard uninspected passenger vessel, 46 U.S.C. 2101 and 8903 | Licensed operator of that type of vessel | Under 100 gross tons, not more than 6 passengers, at least one for hire. |
| Coast Guard small passenger vessel, 46 U.S.C. 2101 and 8902 | Licensed operator for that type and geographic area | Under 100 gross tons, more than 6 passengers, at least one for hire. |
| NOAA charter vessel, 50 CFR 622.2 | The vessel, when it is fishing under a South Atlantic for-hire permit | Under 100 gross tons and subject to the Coast Guard six-or-fewer passenger rule, and it engages in charter fishing. |
| NOAA headboat, 50 CFR 622.2 | The vessel | Valid Coast Guard certificate of inspection to carry more than 6 passengers for hire. |

S.C. Code §50-5-15(10) defines a charter fishing vessel as a vessel used to transport recreational saltwater fishermen for hire. The definition includes charter, party, and head boats. A sightseeing cruise can still be a passenger-for-hire operation under the Coast Guard rules. It needs the SCDNR charter license when the vessel is transporting recreational saltwater fishermen for hire.

The license year in §50-9-15(2) is July 1 through June 30. Section 50-9-20(B) expires a commercial annual license on the last day of the license year for which it was issued. The resident price table checked on this date shows the three annual charter licenses expiring June 30, 2027, with reporting requirements. Point-of-sale agents cannot sell the charter license. Sales go through the department's commercial license channel.

The department's fiscal year 2026–2027 charter summary still cites the fee tiers to §50-9-560(C). Sections 50-9-550 and 50-9-560 are omitted. The enacted fee text is §50-9-540(D). The summary says it does not supersede the Code. Use the Code for the fee.

The blank charter application is the intake field list. It asks for the vessel, capacity, landing, species, and a copy of the captain's Coast Guard credential, and it also asks for personal and business identifiers. A business name on the license requires a FEIN. Those personal fields are not repeated here. Completed applications are not published. There is no separate public data dictionary for a roster.

46 CFR 15.905, opened the same day, is an equivalents section. Specified master, pilot, and mate endorsements authorize service as operator of an uninspected passenger vessel under 100 gross tons, within the endorsement's other limits. The duty to have a licensed operator remains 46 U.S.C. 8903 for that class and 46 U.S.C. 8902 for a small passenger vessel.

South Atlantic snapper-grouper, dolphin and wahoo, and king and Spanish mackerel for-hire permits are Southeast vessel permits. HMS charter/headboat coverage is a separate federal permit for the highly migratory species named by the agency summary. The Southeast permits index that was opened does not publish a South Carolina name roster. The Greater Atlantic public permit lookup was opened only far enough to see that it does not cover this state. Frequently requested Southeast FOIA files were not downloaded.

## Logs and identity

Section 50-5-1915(A) requires a log of persons carried, hours fished, and fish by species, due by the 10th of the next month. The charter summary points operators to VESL for electronic reporting. The VESL homepage retrieved on this date showed no public vessel list and no terms. It is a reporting account, not a roster.

Section 50-5-380(A) says information that directly or indirectly discloses the identity of a licensee or permittee, income, or trade secrets is not public information. The section opens on commercial or scientific take, distribution, or propagation. The charter summary places it immediately after the log rules. Whether the bare license roster is covered is not spelled out. The stated disclosure path in that section is a court order where another law requires confidentiality.

## Landing sample

Crazy Sister Marina in Murrells Inlet sells private fishing charters, ticket fishing, and ocean cruises on separate menus. The same large-boat names are printed on a fishing page and on an ocean-cruise page. A name on both menus is not, by itself, a fishing listing. The cruise page also says passengers may be moved among boats and that vessels may change. Ticket fishing pages that were opened do not assign a hull. Trip caps printed there are marketing limits, not a certificate of inspection.

## Reuse

Silence is recorded as unknown. No page that was opened grants commercial reuse of a charter directory.

- The commercial pricing page footer says all rights reserved and links to the department privacy statement. It does not offer a roster or a reuse license.
- S.C. Code §30-2-50 prohibits a person or private entity from knowingly obtaining or using personal information obtained from a state agency for commercial solicitation directed to a person in this state. Personal information in §30-2-30 includes name, home address, home telephone, photograph, and government identifiers. Commercial solicitation in that section means contact by telephone, mail, or email to sell a consumer product or service. The four stated exceptions do not describe a fishing directory. Whether a directory that does not contact licensees is still solicitation is unresolved. A violation is a misdemeanor. The department privacy statement repeats the statute. FOIA releases remain subject to it.
- FOIA is a written request to the department, with fees under §30-4-30(b). The how-to page was read. No request was submitted. The public FOIA index that was identified is statistics, not a charter name list. A FOIA release would not by itself be a directory grant.
- Statute and federal regulation text can be cited. The eCFR about page says Federal Register material may be reproduced under 1 CFR 2.6, and it labels eCFR text authoritative but unofficial. That is not a permit-file license. Cornell LII pages are a republication of the United States Code. Cite the statute.
- NOAA Fisheries says U.S. government works on its sites are not copyrighted in the United States, encourages non-commercial, scientific, and educational use, and requests the acknowledgement "Courtesy: National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration." The encouragement is a request. External material on those pages keeps its own copyright. Permit-customer files were not taken.
- The Coast Guard snapshot disclaimer limits reliance on completeness and timeliness. It does not grant a commercial directory.
- The marina privacy policy covers information the marina collects from visitors and bookings and says the marina may sell or share data it collects. It is silent on a third-party directory. One operator site reserves all rights. The other operator site had no terms page. Silence is not permission to copy either site.
