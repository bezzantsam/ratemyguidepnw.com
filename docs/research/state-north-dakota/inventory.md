# North Dakota fishing-directory sources and reuse

Checked 2026-11-25. Scope is Devils Lake guide and outfitter evidence, the state license path, and the Devils Lake Basin boating-access list. This note records how the credentials differ, what each publisher states about reuse, and the aggregate result of one defined sample. It does not name people, businesses, license numbers, or client records. Private evidence stays outside this repository.

No one was contacted. Nothing here is a listing, a claim, or a publishing decision.

## Four records, kept apart

A paid fishing trip on Devils Lake can touch four different records. A booking page does not establish any of them.

**Fishing outfitter license.** Century Code section 20.1-01-02 defines an outfitter as a person who holds a business out for hire, provides facilities or services for consideration, pays for the use of land and is paid by a third party for that use, or uses equipment or accommodations for outdoor recreation, including fishing on lakes, reservoirs, rivers, and streams. An outfitter may act as a guide. The same definitions section treats a business entity as a person. Section 20.1-03-36.1 sets the fishing outfitter fee at $250 for a resident and $500 for a nonresident, or $125 and $250 when the applicant has already paid for a hunting guide or hunting outfitter license in that license year. Section 20.1-03-37 requires liability insurance of $100,000 per individual and $300,000 per accident for a hunting outfitter or a fishing outfitter. Section 20.1-03-36.2 says a fishing outfitter license is not transferable and expires on March 31.

**Hunting guide license.** This is the only guide class. Section 20.1-01-02 defines a guide as an individual employed by, or contracting with, a licensed outfitter to furnish personal services for an activity the employing outfitter is licensed to conduct. Section 20.1-03-36.1 sets a separate hunting-guide fee of $100 for a resident and $400 for a nonresident. Administrative Code chapter 30-04-03 and the department exam notice dated November 24, 2026 describe the written exam, a background check, adult CPR, standard first aid, and employment by or a contract with a licensed hunting outfitter. The notice does not state a fishing-outfitter exam. Section 20.1-03-38 places the exam on hunting guide and hunting outfitter applicants. A role label on a booking page, including "head guide" or "owner," displays neither license.

**Booking agent.** Section 20.1-03-36.1(4) places three situations outside the license requirement: services on real property the person owns or leases for the primary pursuit of bona fide agricultural interests; a nonprofit registered with the secretary of state; and a booking agent for a legal outfitter. The booking agent receives only a fee for referring or marketing that outfitter. A state or federal criminal game or fish conviction within three years, or a suspended or revoked hunt or fish license, removes the exemption. The director must determine exempt acres by county and publish the results. No such results page was linked from the handbook, the regulations index, or the code PDF. A shop name can be a booking desk. The shop is the licensee only when a license record says so.

**The water, the landing, and the hull.** The 2026-28 fishing guide lists basin access sites. A listed ramp is an access site. It becomes an operator's departure only when that operator's own page names it. Section 20.1-01-02 defines "boat" for the wildlife title as a watercraft used for noncommercial purposes, a watercraft leased, rented, or chartered for someone else's noncommercial use, or a watercraft carrying six or fewer passengers. That definition is a statutory category. It is not a commercial-hull registry. The fishing guide says any watercraft propelled by a motor must have a valid registration, and that the certificate of number is not transferable. No registration lookup was queried. A described fleet of ice houses is not a vessel.

Section 20.1-03-36 says an individual may not act as a guide or outfitter, or advertise as one, without first securing a license. The check date falls inside a license year that would run through March 31, 2027 only for a license that was issued. No license status was verified.

The handbook landing, last updated July 21, 2025, repeats the statutory guide and outfitter definitions. It states that the law creates a hunting outfitter and a fishing outfitter, that one person may hold both, and that the only guide class is a hunting guide. The page labels the handbook as educational material. The Century Code PDF is the controlling text.

The official chapter PDF retrieved on the check date includes the 2025 fishing-outfitter fees. In section 20.1-03-37(7), a hunting guide or hunting outfitter applicant must have legally hunted for part of each of any three years in a manner that contributes to experience and competency. The retrieved subsection does not add an in-state limit. Fishing outfitter applicants are outside that hunting-experience sentence. Administrative Code 30-04-03-14, amended effective April 1, 2026, requires a hunting outfitter applicant to show two years as a hunting guide in North Dakota or another state. The code PDF remains the authority for who must be licensed and for the insurance amounts.

## What was available, and what was not

The specialty-license address, `https://apps.nd.gov/gnf/onlineservices/lic/public/myGNF.htm`, presented an Identify Customer form asking for first name, last name, and date of birth. That form identifies a customer account. It is not a public outfitter roster and not a public status search. No name was submitted. No licensee row was retrieved. The roster count is null.

No download of fishing-outfitter or hunting-guide licenses was posted on the handbook landing, the regulations index, or the exam notice. No records request was filed. Administrative Code 30-04-03-19 requires a licensed outfitter to keep a hunt or fish record for each client for at least three years. The minimum fields include the client's name, address, and hunting or fishing license number, and, for a fish record, the water body and the county. Those client records were not requested. Section 20.1-03-38 makes trade secret or proprietary information submitted on a separate form confidential, and it allows release of that category only as a non-identifying aggregate. The client-log rule, as read, does not itself call the log confidential.

The 2026-28 fishing guide is effective April 1, 2026 through March 31, 2028. Its Devils Lake System Boating Access section says it lists all boating access sites in the Devils Lake Basin: Lake Irvine, Devils Lake, and Stump Lake. The printed list contains 12 sites. Each site has a boat ramp, a courtesy dock, and restrooms unless the entry says otherwise. The guide says runoff and lake level can make a ramp marginal or unusable, and it tells the reader to check the department site or the managing authority. Game and Fish manages three of the 12. East Bay is one of those three, on ND Highway 20 south of Devils Lake, with a paved access road, security lights, and trash receptacles. The East Bay entry does not list boat rental. One other entry on the same list does list boat rental, under a concession manager. That concession page was not opened and is not a sample row. Parks and Recreation manages two sites. County, community, and concession managers account for the rest.

The guide's separate county index of fishing waters excludes this basin list and points to the where-to-fish application for a more complete list of those other waters. That application was not opened. Twelve is the count of sites printed in the basin section. It is not a count of ramps confirmed usable on the check date.

The guide's boating section states the motorboat registration rule and points to `gf.nd.gov/boating/safety-regulations` for the complete boating regulations. That address now resolves to the boating regulations index, which was not opened. An aquatic nuisance species sticker is required for a motorized watercraft that is not licensed in North Dakota. The sticker is not a charter registry. Wildlife management area rules, last updated August 2026, require a lease or permit from the director before a person conducts a commercial enterprise on a wildlife management area, and they say a watercraft may not be left unattended there unless it is used daily. That land-use permit is separate from an outfitter license and from a data license.

One additional operator homepage failed before it rendered. The failure was recorded after one attempt and the page was not retried. The sample uses the Devils Lake pages that rendered and the department access list.

A Department of Tourism category page for Devils Lake guides and outfitters appeared in search results. A direct fetch of that category page returned a challenge screen and did not render the list. No directory total was verified. The category page is not a Game and Fish roster.

## Reuse

The department policy page says records on the website follow the same controls as records requested in person, and that they are open to the public on request unless a specific law provides otherwise. The page disclaims accuracy, reliability, and timeliness, and it disclaims endorsement of linked content. The privacy section covers information a visitor chooses to provide. The page states no commercial-directory grant, no attribution line for license status, no bulk license feed, and no permission desk for a directory.

The statewide privacy policy on nd.gov covers executive-branch websites. It says a visitor generally does not have to provide personal information in order to view or download public pages. No revision date was printed on the page that was read. The policy does not grant commercial reuse of license status.

The tourism privacy policy was last updated August 4, 2021. It says the site does not collect personal information unless a visitor provides it, and that submitted personal information is not disclosed to third parties unless the law requires disclosure. It also says North Dakota public-records law makes information received in connection with state business available on request unless a statutory exemption applies. It does not grant commercial reuse of business listings.

Section 20.1-03-38 limits the separate trade-secret form to confidential treatment and to a non-identifying aggregate release. That limit is not a directory license, and it is not a finding that ordinary license status is confidential. The official text of Century Code section 44-04-18 was not opened, so this note states no copyright rule for department records.

The fishing guide says it is for information and is not a complete listing of regulations. That sentence is not a commercial-directory grant. The operator pages that rendered are silent on commercial directory reuse. Silence here is an unknown.

No source checked on this pass grants bulk commercial reuse of a named license list. No list was downloaded.

## Unresolved

The permission question is whether Game and Fish and the state will authorize commercial-directory reuse of license-status facts and access-site facts. The department policy page describes request access. It does not grant that reuse. The most useful next primary source is the Century Code chapter that contains the open-records statute, [t44c04.pdf](https://ndlegis.gov/cencode/t44c04.pdf). That file was not opened.

Also unresolved: the number of current fishing outfitter licenses and hunting guide licenses. The customer form was not tested with a name, because no name was submitted. The exempt-acre results required by section 20.1-03-36.1 were not found. Current usability of the 12 printed basin ramps is unknown. No sampled operator named a departure ramp. Whether the boating regulations page exposes a hull or registration lookup is unknown. No hull in the sample is tied to a name, a documentation number, or an owner.
