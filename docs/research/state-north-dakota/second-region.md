# Lake Sakakawea sources and reuse

Checked 2026-11-25. This note adds Lake Sakakawea to the North Dakota research pass. Statewide license rules, the Devils Lake sample, and the Devils Lake Basin access count stay in [inventory.md](inventory.md). Counts below are incremental. This note does not name people, businesses, license numbers, or client records. Private evidence stays outside this repository.

No one was contacted. Nothing here is a listing, a claim, or a publishing decision.

## What this pass checked

The guide and outfitter handbook landing was opened again. It is still marked last updated July 21, 2025. The page is educational material for people seeking a license. It contains no Lake Sakakawea outfitter list and no licensee roster. Department pages carry a footer line, "Copyright 2025. All rights reserved, the state of North Dakota." The policy page still describes open-records access and states no commercial-directory grant.

The 2026-28 fishing guide, effective April 1, 2026 through March 31, 2028, prints a Missouri River System boating-access section. The PDF file was modified August 24, 2026. The section introduces "a list of boating access sites on the Missouri River System in North Dakota." It does not use the basin section's "all sites" wording, and it does not print a separate Lake Sakakawea total. The numbered entries run from 1 through 57. The default, unless an entry says otherwise, is a boat ramp, a courtesy dock, and restrooms. Entry 29 is Lake Sakakawea State Park, just east and north of Pick City, managed by North Dakota Parks and Recreation, with boat rental and an access fee among the printed facilities. Boat rental on that line is a facility flag. It is not a named hull and not an outfitter.

The Parks and Recreation boat-ramp page says Lake Sakakawea State Park has two ramps: a multi-lane ramp on the west side and a single-lane ramp on the southeast side, with courtesy docks at both. The same page says ramps are available seasonally and tells the reader to check with the park in fall and spring. The park page says the ramps offer deep-water access. The marina page says the marina's functional elevation range is 1805 to 1855 feet and that 74 slips from 24 to 72 feet are rented by the season. A slip length is not a hull. A phone and an email on the park pages were not stored.

The department boating-access page, headed "2026 Boat Ramps and Facilities," links an Upper Missouri River / Lake Sakakawea map in the where-to-fish application. It says a ramp marked usable on the site can still be closed after a flood or heavy rain. The Lake Sakakawea application address returned HTTP 302 to the where-to-fish results script. That script holds access records for many waters. Filtering to the location text "Sakakawea, Lake" yields 77 records whose type is a ramp. On the check date those 77 labels read: 24 Usable, 36 Not Usable, 4 Unusable, 1 Marginal, 1 Steep - Use Caution, and 11 telling the reader to contact the managing entity. One of the 11 uses a misspelled label. Contact names and phone numbers in the script were not stored.

Inside that set, Lake Sakakawea State Park has two poured-concrete ramp labels. The script marks Main Ramp usable and Rodeo Bay Ramp not usable, and it names Parks and Recreation as the manager. The parks page does not use the names Main Ramp or Rodeo Bay. Those two labels were not joined to "west" and "southeast." The usable flag is a same-department status on the check date, read with the boating-access warning above.

A November 2, 2026 department release says low water on Lake Sakakawea, Lake Oahe, and the Missouri River is projected to make 2027 boating access difficult, and that many ramps and parking areas will be less than ideal even after agency work. It also says no further open-water fishing tournaments will be added for 2027 beyond those already approved in the 2026 scheduling cycle. The release is not a ramp-by-ramp closure list and not an outfitter roster. It names no corps project URL.

An October 27, 2026 department release says zebra mussels were confirmed on the extreme eastern end of Lake Sakakawea, with two adults along the northern shoreline near the Highway 83 embankment, and that rules would be refined over the winter. The department's infested-waters page, read the same day, lists curlyleaf pondweed for Lake Sakakawea in Dunn, McKenzie, McLean, Mercer, Mountrail, and Williams counties. Its zebra-mussel confirmed list does not name Lake Sakakawea. The two pages are both department pages. They do not say the same thing about zebra mussels. Neither page is an outfitter list. An aquatic-nuisance sticker remains a watercraft requirement, not a charter registry.

## Sample

The bookable sample is five rows from pages that rendered: four operator identities and one vessel row. Sampled rows equal supported plus held, 5 = 0 + 5.

All four operator rows are held. No page shows a fishing-outfitter number or a hunting-guide number. A role word on a booking page, including owner, captain, or guide, is the page's label. Each page is silent on a boat name, a documentation number, and a registration number. No page names a departure ramp that was also confirmed by a second publisher. One page states a length class of boat. A marketplace page for a different operation states a length and a builder and a four-passenger capacity. A third page states a multi-decade history on this water and places ice trips on Devils Lake. A fourth page carries a 2026 date on a sentence saying that season's guiding on Lake Sakakawea had ended, and the page's own modified date is September 22, 2021. None of these is a hull. None of these businesses is one of the Devils Lake businesses in the inventory sample. Pages that also name Devils Lake were kept as separate businesses. No inventory observation ID was linked, because the names do not match.

One marketplace page repeats one operator's business, captain label, and three waters. That is one independent publisher match for the business and the role. It is not a match for a license number or a hull. The operator's own page does not state the boat description that appears on the marketplace page. The two descriptions were not joined.

The vessel row is held. No sampled page identifies one physical hull. Length, builder, passenger capacity, and marina slip size were left as type or facility facts.

Lake Sakakawea State Park is a separate access-site row. The fishing guide and the Parks and Recreation boat-ramp page agree that the park has boat-ramp access on the lake, managed by Parks and Recreation. Sampled access rows from the printed system list equal supported plus held, 1 = 1 + 0. The printed roster count is 57 Missouri River System entries, not a Sakakawea-only census. Independent publisher matches for this park are 1. The where-to-fish count of 77 Sakakawea ramp records is a different list. Those 77 records were counted and were not turned into 77 rows. The park's two application labels are notes on this one access row.

Two operator hosts returned an HTTP 403 challenge page and were not used as observations. A tourism listing fetch in this pass returned the same class of challenge page. One other homepage rendered and does not name Lake Sakakawea, so it is not in the sample. One guessed host returned an unrelated commercial page and was discarded.

## Reuse

Department, statewide disclaimer, and parks pages were read for a commercial-directory grant. The department footer reserves copyright. The policy page describes request access and states no directory grant. The state disclaimer page, with a 2020 copyright footer, contains no commercial-reproduction sentence in the text that was extracted. Parks pages point at that disclaimer and at the statewide privacy policy. Silence on a directory grant is an unknown.

One operator site states that copying or downloading site content without express written permission is prohibited. That sentence covers that site. It is not a state license rule. Other operator pages that rendered are silent on commercial-directory reuse. The marketplace terms address is linked from the charter page. The terms document that downloaded did not yield a readable directory clause, and the footer reserves copyright. Reuse of that page is unknown.

No source checked on this pass grants bulk commercial reuse of a named license list. No license list was downloaded. No records request was sent.

## Unresolved

The permission question is whether Game and Fish and the state will authorize commercial-directory reuse of license-status facts and access-site facts. The most useful next primary source is still the unopened open-records chapter, [t44c04.pdf](https://ndlegis.gov/cencode/t44c04.pdf).

Also unresolved: which of the 57 printed system sites sit on Lake Sakakawea, and which application ramp name is the west ramp at Lake Sakakawea State Park. The park map linked from the park page was not opened: [Lake Sakakawea State Park main map](https://www.parkrec.nd.gov/sites/www/files/documents/Destinations/Lake%20Sakakawea/lssp_main_map.pdf). Current usability of the printed system list is unknown. The application status is a check-date flag, and the department warns that a usable flag can be stale. The zebra-mussel news and the infested-waters list do not agree. The ANS landing linked from the news release was not opened. No sampled operator named a documentation number, a registration, or a confirmed departure ramp. No fishing-outfitter roster was found on the pages opened for this lake.
