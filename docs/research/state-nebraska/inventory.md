# Nebraska fishing-directory inventory

Checked 2026-11-25. Phase `nebraska-inventory`. The water in this pass is Lake McConaughy. This note is the public method: which credential exists, what each publisher allows a commercial directory to do, and the aggregate result of a four-row guide sample. Business names, guide names, hull names, phone numbers, email addresses, and street addresses stay outside this repository.

No one was contacted. This file is not a listing and not a publishing decision.

## What is licensed

A Nebraska fishing permit is the angler's own permit. It is not a guide credential. The starting page for this pass, special occasion permits, is written permission for an event, tournament, or commercial production in a state park or wildlife management area. It is not a roster of fishing guides and it does not list holders.

Neb. Rev. Stat. 37-356, Laws 2024, LB867, section 2, defines a guide as a person who advertises or holds themselves out for hire to provide hunting or fishing services, and an outfitter as a person who provides facilities, equipment, accommodations, or other services for hunting or fishing. The same section says the Game and Parks Commission may keep a voluntary hunting and fishing guide and outfitter database on its website, may charge a registration fee, and may list an applicant for three years if the applicant meets the statutory conditions. Those conditions include liability insurance or similar security and a business registered in Nebraska. Hunter education is not required for fishing guides. The section does not require a person to register before guiding. A path guessed from that statute, `/permits/guide-and-outfitter-database/` on outdoornebraska.gov, returned the agency's 404 page on this date. No public lookup, download, or field dictionary for that database was found.

On state park property, 163 Nebraska Administrative Code, chapter 5, section 001, as opened on the Cornell Law School compilation, prohibits commercial soliciting without a permit, with an exception for transactions with authorized concessionaires. The same section allows vending of goods and services on park lands only with prior written authorization of the Commission, and it requires written authorization before a park area is used as a headquarters for commercial or professional activities. That compilation is not the Secretary of State rule filing. No holder list for those park authorizations was posted on the pages opened.

The Commission's water-trails page says the Commission does not regulate or endorse river outfitters and keeps a convenience list for canoe, kayak, and float trips. The linked PDF, filed under a September 2026 path, has 30 business entries. The file's first page has no revision date. None of the entries is a Lake McConaughy fishing guide. The page and the PDF contain contact details. Those details were not copied. The site copyright policy requires written permission to reproduce website materials. It allows linking, and it allows download of educational or informational materials without a separate permission. That download sentence is not a grant to republish a commercial directory.

## Lake McConaughy access

The Lake McConaughy State Recreation Area page describes a reservoir of about 30,000 surface acres near Ogallala, managed by the Commission. On this date the page said public boat-ramp access was no longer available because of low water and directed visitors to private launch services. An August 7, 2026 news release names three vendors for that launch service. The park page also names concession areas for food, bait, and boat rental. Those names are the agency's own facility directions. They are not a guide roster, not a hull inventory, and not a count of written vending authorizations. Phone numbers on those pages were not copied. The visitor-center address on the park page is a park office.

## Reuse

A current page, a download, and authority to claim a listing are three different things.

| Publisher | Reuse status | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nebraska Game and Parks Commission website | conditional | Copyright page: website materials require written permission to reproduce. Linking is allowed. Downloadable educational or informational materials and lesson plans may be downloaded without permission. No commercial-directory grant. The Commission disclaims accuracy and says a commercial name on the site is not an endorsement. |
| River outfitter PDF | conditional | Same site policy. The PDF says the list is for convenience and is not an endorsement. Contact fields were not copied. |
| Nebraska Legislature statute page | not_granted | Footer: copyright, all rights reserved. The disclaimer speaks to accuracy and points readers to the printed official publication. It does not grant directory reuse. |
| Cornell compilation of 163 NAC chapter 5 | unknown | The rule text was read there. That host's terms were not opened. |
| Four operator publishers | not_granted (3), unknown (1) | Three pages carry a 2026 all-rights-reserved line and no directory license. One older site presented no terms URL. |
| Marketplace listing | unknown | The listing was opened. Its legal terms were not. |

The highest-priority permission question is whether the Commission will give written permission before any guide, outfitter, concession, or convenience-list name is republished, and whether it will publish the voluntary 37-356 database at all. A 404, a convenience list, and an operator's own booking page do not authorize a commercial directory to claim a listing.

## Where to start

Read 37-356, then look for an actual database page and a rule that implements it. Until that page exists, a Lake McConaughy guide is an operator claim plus whatever written park authorization covers soliciting or vending on the recreation area. The river-outfitter PDF does not answer the fishing-guide question. Add a hull only when a public record names that hull.
