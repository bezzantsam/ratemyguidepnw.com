# Minnesota fishing-charter inventory

Checked 2026-11-25. Scope is the Lake Superior fishing-guide license and Lake Superior fishing trips that depart from Duluth. This note is methodology, aggregate counts, primary-source links, and unresolved permission questions. It does not identify operators or people.

## Credential

The assigned lead is a Department of Natural Resources information sheet dated January 24, 2018, last modified that day. The current requirement is in the 2026 compilation of Minnesota Statutes, section 97C.311. A person may not operate a charter boat and guide anglers for compensation on Lake Superior or the St. Louis River Estuary without a fishing guide license. For that section and for the fee section, the estuary is the Coast Guard navigable waters of the lower St. Louis River to the Minnesota State Highway No. 23 bridge. The commissioner is to adopt rules for issuance, qualifications, record keeping, and reporting. The watercraft must comply with applicable Coast Guard licensing and safety requirements. That watercraft sentence is not a state hull license. History on the section runs through 1999.

The fee, section 97A.475, subdivision 15, is $125 for a resident and $400 for a nonresident. If another state charges a Minnesota resident more than $440 for the same license, the nonresident fee for a resident of that state is the greater fee.

Rule 6262.3000, published electronically June 11, 2008, says an applicant must be at least 18 when the license is issued and must hold a Coast Guard operator license appropriate for the watercraft. The application goes to the Lake Superior Area Fisheries Supervisor. Rule 6262.3050, published the same day, requires monthly records to that supervisor by the tenth day of the next month, including months with no guiding, except November through April unless guiding occurred. Three missed submissions in two years can invalidate a later application for up to three years. The rules do not publish the reports.

The 2018 sheet says the Department licenses this activity only on Lake Superior and the estuary, and that a Coast Guard six-passenger operator license is a prerequisite. It also describes sole-state waters: six or fewer passengers with no state license or inspection, and more than six with a boat master license. Those sole-state sentences were not restated on the current Labor and Industry pages. The sheet prints agency contact lines. They were not copied.

A different statute, section 326B.94, covers inland waters and more than six passengers for hire. It requires an annual boat permit unless the boat has a current Coast Guard inspection certificate, and a boat master license unless the operator holds a Coast Guard charter boat captain's license. The Labor and Industry boat master page states a $50 exam fee, an $80 license fee, an $80 renewal, and a $120 late renewal, on a two-year term. Its boats-for-hire page states a $200 annual inspection fee as new in 2021. A guessed boat-master path returned HTTP 404. The live pages were used instead. Section 326B.092, which the statute cites for fees, was not opened. This inland license is not the Lake Superior fishing-guide license.

## Public list

No public roster, name lookup, or download of fishing-guide licensees was found. The commercial catalog contains 32 license-type objects. One code string is used on two different names, so a code is not a unique key. The guide object is one row. Its fee field says Varies. Its description states $125 and $400 and says the license is for guiding anglers on Lake Superior or its tributaries. That water wording is wider than the statute's Lake Superior or defined estuary, and the description does not state the reciprocity clause. The row has no link and no PDF. The issued count is unknown. Monthly reports to the fisheries supervisor are not this list.

## Duluth departure list

No city marina charter roster was found. The departure source that was open is the Visit Duluth fishing and sailing directory. It printed 30 results, and the page markup contains 30 listing cards. The footer copyright is 2026, all rights reserved. No card date was found.

Seven cards describe a Lake Superior fishing charter. Two other cards describe a fishing guide service and do not describe a measured charter boat. One of those two says inland, fly, and ice fishing. The other names no boat. The remaining 21 cards are paddling, sailing, rafting, a park, a visitor center, a shop, marina or boat rental, or a sightseeing cruise. They were not sampled as charter hulls.

Five of the seven charter cards were compared with a current first-party page. One was supported. Four were held. Sampled = supported + held: 5 = 1 + 4.

The supported row is a current fishing page and the directory card that agree on one hull name, a length, a make, and a beam. No registration or documentation number was on those pages. The operator page has no content date. The directory date is unknown. Agreement on the check date is not a department license and not a marina slip assignment.

One held row has a length and a make on the operator page. The directory card names the business and does not state the hull name or the measurement. A shared business is not a hull.

One held row is named on both pages. Neither page states a length, a make, or a registration number.

One held row has a length and a make on the operator page. The directory card does not state that name or that measurement, and it names both Lake Superior and the St. Louis River.

One held row is a booking desk. The directory card states six boats of one length and make and names no hull. The operator page says six boats and then names seven. Its length does not match the directory card. A shared class sentence was not used as a separate hull. A second host of the same business was not counted as a second publisher. That page says the captains hold Coast Guard and Minnesota licenses and names an association. It does not link an association roster. A self-described license was not treated as a department record.

One charter host returned HTTP 200 with a 111-byte body on two attempts. The file was last modified January 22, 2018. It was left outside the five. The directory card for that host states a length and a make and also offers pleasure trips. The directory alone was not used.

One charter card was not opened. The two fishing-guide cards that are not charter-boat rows were not opened.

## Commercial reuse

A license requirement, a public list, and authority to copy either one into a commercial directory are separate. No public license list was found.

- The Department copyright notice allows personal viewing and printing and fair use. Commercial publication, including publication on other websites, requires permission. The notice is not a directory grant. It cites Minnesota Statutes chapter 13. That chapter was not opened.
- The Revisor page about the statutes is silent on commercial reuse. Silence is unknown.
- The Labor and Industry disclaimer says the information and documents on its website are in the public domain, requests attribution when text is pulled, and asks other sites to link to its documents rather than host them. That sentence covers the inland boat pages. It does not cover the Department guide list, and it does not authorize a claim that a person is listed.
- The Visit Duluth terms allow viewing, printing, and personal sharing for travel planning. Republication on the web or in print requires permission. The directory footer reserves all rights. Linking is encouraged. That is not a directory grant.
- Of the operator pages read, one reserves all rights. The others say nothing about reuse. Silence is unknown. A copyright line is not a directory license. A booking form on an operator site is not a third-party grant.

Nothing checked here authorizes this directory to claim a listing.
